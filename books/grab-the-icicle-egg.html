Read the opening pages

One giant ox. One icy bridge. Three black banners waiting in the dark.

It's update day, and a brand-new zone called Shiver Ridge has opened. Its guardian, Big Shaggy, is a musk ox the size of a hill, and he can out-run anyone who carries his egg. Even the server's fastest player.

Ten-year-old Zeke Delgado and his gaming squad, the Speedy Yolks, go in anyway. His big sister Abril has a plan for every step. His best friend Kiyo knows every number. Abuela Toña, a retired umpire, has the slowest speed on the server and the loudest "SAFE!" in the kitchen.

Then the sky goes dark, and the Night Nabbers arrive: three silent players who wait at the end of the only bridge home and grab whatever you drop. They play hard, they play fair, and they already know the squad's best trick. When the rarest egg on the mountain appears, Abril must do something she has never done: change the plan mid-run.

Read the opening pages

ROLL the tape. Slow it way down.

That’s SirSizzle, the fastest player anybody on Base Row has ever seen. White hoodie, hood up, upside down. They’re flying over a long ditch full of snow, and they’re carrying nothing at all.

They landed headfirst in a snowbank at the bottom of the ditch, with only their sneakers sticking out.

“What happened?” said Abril. “Kiyo. What happened?”

Kiyo was standing right at the gate line, looking in. You can’t go through the gate without the speed, but you can see.

“There’s a bridge,” he said. “Right behind the gate. It’s made of ice, and it goes over that ditch. On the other side, there are steps going up, and a nest at the top. SirSizzle grabbed a blue egg and came down the steps. And then…”

Then I saw it for myself.

Something came down the steps on the far side of the bridge. It was a hill of dark brown fur, shaggy all the way to the ground. Two horns curled down beside its face like the handlebars on a bike. A fringe of white frost hung across its forehead. It was built from big glossy blocks, like everything in the game, but it was so huge the blocks looked like boulders.

It walked onto the bridge, scooped a blue egg off the ice with its horns, and carried it back up the steps.

Then it lay down by the nest, and a puff of frost floated up from its nose. Then another. A blocky “Zzz” drifted over its head.

“Okay, so,” said Kiyo, very softly. “I’m naming him Big Shaggy.”

“He caught SirSizzle,” I said. “SirSizzle has a speed with commas on its commas.”

“With an egg,” said Kiyo. “Everybody is slower with an egg. Fast enough to get in is not fast enough to get out. That’s my rule. Big Shaggy is my rule with horns.”

Down in the ditch, SirSizzle climbed out of the snowbank and shook the snow off their hood. Then two letters popped up in chat.

SirSizzle: gg

“Who are they saying that to?” I asked. “There’s nobody there.”

“The ox, mijo,” said Abuela. “They’re saying good game to the ox. That’s a good loser.”

(In real life, Kiyo wrote that down on an index card.)

Next, MegaYoink went in, through the gate in a purple blur. Thirty seconds later, he came flying out over the ditch, yelling in all caps. He landed right next to SirSizzle. They lay there in the snow like two very unhappy snow angels.

SCOREBOARD: BIG SHAGGY 2, FAMOUS PLAYERS 0.

Then my chat went ding.

MegaYoink: trade u a ridge egg for ur pigeon

MegaYoink: when i get one
