Read the opening pages

Every morning, the ice swan is a little smaller. And every night, the frozen garden has one more statue than anybody carved.

The Grand Lantern Hotel has closed for its quiet week, so most of the hotel family has crossed a frozen lake to the Frostpine Lodge, its little sister inn in the snowy north. Twelve ice sculptures stand in the lodge's garden, waiting for Winter Lantern Night. But every morning at sunrise, the gate lantern is sitting on the snow, and the Swan has melted a little more. Nobody owns up to touching either.

Yuki Tanabe counts everything: lanterns, sculptures, steps to the gate. Her grandmother keeps the lodge, and she has asked for Yuki by name. But Yuki never says what she thinks until she is sure, and this time, waiting could leave a boy alone in the dark, with something pale standing among the ice.

With Nettie, who notices people, Monty, who can get anyone talking, and little Rory, who is sure every creak is a ghost, Yuki has until First Light, at midnight on Winter Lantern Night.

A spine-tingling winter hotel mystery where every clue works the way it does in Minecraft, with a Spot-the-Clue Answer Key at the back.

Read the opening pages

TEO Holm ate his soup with his coat buttoned to the chin.

Yuki noticed it the way she noticed a lantern out of place. The hall was warm and the fire was big, and everyone else had hung their coats by the door. Eleven coats hung there, and Teo’s wasn’t one of them.

Teo sat at the very end of the long table, and his father sat at the other end, by the window. Between them sat everybody else.

Nettie saw it too, and she tapped her cap brim twice.

“Huh,” she said softly. “Look at that.”

Monty stood up with a whole pumpkin pie in his hands. His dad had baked six of them that morning and carried them all the way north in a chest.

“New slogan,” Monty announced. “The Frostpine Lodge: Cold Hands, Warm Pie!”

“No,” said Yuki.

“You didn’t even think about it.”

“I didn’t need to.”

Monty went down the table with the pie, a slice for every guest. Talking to people was his favorite thing in the world, and he talked to every one of them. Mr. Pike got a slice and a question about maps. Mrs. Cragg got a slice and a question about packed ice, and she answered it for a very long time.

Mr. Holm got a slice, and he didn’t hear the question at all.

At the end of the table, Monty set a slice in front of Teo. “Hi! I’m Monty, room service, usually. How long have you been here?”

Teo looked at the pie. “Seven days,” he said. “Dad cuts ice on the lake every morning, and then he carves it all day.”

Yuki picked up her soup bowl and sat down in the empty chair across from him. She didn’t say hello, because Yuki almost never said hello.

“You count in fours,” she said.

Teo looked up. His eyes were gray, like the lake. “Four is a good number,” he said. “It’s a square.”

“Five is better for lanterns. One for each finger.”

“Four,” said Teo. “Four corners, and four sides of a block.”

They looked at each other across the table. Yuki had never met anybody who argued about numbers before.

“How far is it from here to the lake gate?” she asked. She meant it as a test, because nobody knew things like that.

“A hundred and twenty-four steps from the garden door,” said Teo at once. “Thirty-one fours.”

Yuki wrote it down. She felt a strange, small bump inside her, like missing a step in the dark.

“When did you count it?” she asked.

Teo looked down at his pie.

At the other end of the table, his father stood up and went to the window. “After the ice, Teo,” he said, though Teo hadn’t asked him anything at all.
