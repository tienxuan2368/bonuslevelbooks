Read the opening pages

One evening, every look in Spawnbury falls off. Now the whole town is two thousand bacon hairs, and nobody can find anybody.

Nolan Tibbs has always worn the plain Starter Look, with brown hair in three flat strips that folks call bacon hair. Everybody always asks if he just got here. Now everybody looks just like him, including his little sister, Tinsley, who was picked to lead the Shine Parade and is sure nobody will see her without her sparkle.

It gets worse. Loosebolt, the rule-bending visitor who loves watching things fall, looks like everyone else too. A glass swan float rolls toward the Edge. A crowd follows the wrong waving bacon hair into Jellybean Park, onto a ride where the floor drops into the sky one row at a time. And Tinsley is stuck on the last island of candy tiles, with one plain brick in her pocket and a jump she isn't sure she can make.

Read the opening pages

Here is a thing nobody tells you about the coldest week of winter in Spawnbury: it’s the shiniest week of the year.

The sun goes down early, and the nights stretch long and blue. So the whole town throws a party about it. We call it Shine Week. Every glow shop on the Shine Mile turns its lights up as bright as they go. On the fifth day, at sundown, the floats roll down the Mile in the Shine Parade.

That morning there were snow bricks on every roof, the Welcome Hut smelled like cocoa, and there were five days to go.

My little sister was already wearing all of it.

“DO I SPARKLE?” Tinsley yelled.

She was standing on the Hut counter, which she isn’t allowed to do. She had glitter arms, glitter legs, a glitter body and glitter on her glitter. Her sparkly headband was on, because it’s always on. She’s six and has exactly one volume, which is loud.

“You sparkle,” I said. “Get off the counter.”

“HOW MUCH?”

“A lot. Off the counter, Tins.”

“Welcome!” said Mom, coming through the back door with a clipboard of maps. She says that to everybody, including people who live here. “Tinsley, down. Nolan, the pad’s humming.”

It was. The Spawn Pad sits right outside our door, and it hums like a fridge thinking hard when somebody new is coming.

Hmmmm.

I walked out, because you walk to the pad and never run. Running scares fresh spawns.

A boy popped up in the middle of the glow, inside his shimmery ten-second bubble. He had the Starter Look, with brown hair in three flat, wavy strips that stick out like bacon in a pan. He had a plain cream shirt and plain blue pants. It’s the look everybody arrives in, and folks call anybody who wears it a bacon hair.

It’s also my look. I’m Nolan Tibbs, I’m eleven, and I’ve never taken it off.

The name over the boy’s head said Oskar. When his bubble popped, he looked at the snow, then at the glowing street across the square, then at me.

“Hi, Oskar,” I said. “Welcome to Spawnbury.” I held out a map.

He took it and stared at my hair. “Did you just get here?”

“Eleven years ago.”

(That makes 2,345. I keep count, because somebody has to.)

I did my wave, the plain one. Everybody gets seven free moves, and the wave is the best of them. Oskar waved back.

On our doorstep lay Pancake, our dog, who is small and square and the color of butter, with one darker ear. When he’s happy, he lies so flat he looks like a doormat, and Oskar wiped his feet on him.
