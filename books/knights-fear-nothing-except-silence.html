Read the opening pages

The Bell of Bellford cannot be made by any smith in the world. Two days before the Harvest Fair, it is gone.

Pebblecrown is the smallest kingdom in the blocky world, with one castle, one village and three squires who want to be knights more than anything. Loud Hildy quotes the Squire's Code at everyone. Careful Kiran reads banners the way other kids read books. Gentle, enormous Alfie apologizes to everything, even the monsters.

When the bell vanishes, Kiran builds a perfect case against the realm's boastful young knight, Sir Cuthbert. It is neat, clever and completely wrong. Now Kiran has to say so in front of the whole village, and then follow the real clues: a creeper in the dark, a missing lantern, and something at the bottom of the village well that used to be a zombie and will never let go.

And the lanterns are down. On the darkest night of the year, twenty zombies can rise inside Bellford's own fences, all at once.

A thrilling, funny quest novel for kids 9-12, set in a Minecraft kingdom where every bell, creeper, drowned and iron golem works just like the game. Short chapters, no gore, battles against the game's monsters only, and a story about honesty and teamwork: the bravest sound in the world is someone saying "I was wrong."

Read the opening pages

Hear now the tale of the Bell of Bellford, which no smith in the blocky world could ever make, and of the cold autumn morning when it was gone.

Pebblecrown was the smallest kingdom there ever was. It had one castle on one hill, one village called Bellford at the bottom of the hill, one river ford, one horse, a great many sheep, and one bell. The village was named after the bell. The bell was older than anyone could remember. It hung from a block of stone bricks beside the village well, and every morning since Midsummer, somebody from the castle walked down the hill to ring it.

This was the Steward’s idea. After the raid at Midsummer, he had decided that Bellford should practice. So every sunrise, one squire rang the bell. Every villager ran home to bed. And after a polite little while, every villager came out again. They did not seem to mind. Some of them seemed to enjoy it.

This morning it was Kiran’s turn.

Kiran Mehta walked down the hill road in the gray before sunrise with his book under his arm and a stick of charcoal behind his ear. He was eleven, with neat black hair except for one tuft that never lay down, and a blue tunic with a pocket full of dyes. He liked mornings, because nobody rushed you in the morning.

He looked up. The clouds were coming out of the dark in long, flat bands.

“Fess,” he said. “Fess. Fess. Per Fess Inverted.” He wrote them down.

Behold Kiran the Sky-Reader of Pebblecrown, herald of the realm, whose eyes—

“I don’t read the sky,” said Kiran. “I look at clouds. Everybody can look at clouds.”

The Chronicler withdraws “Sky-Reader.” Kiran was looking at clouds.

Behind him came a sound like a minecart falling down a flight of stairs. That was Hildy.

Hildegard Brask came down the hill at a run. Her two sandy-red braids stuck out sideways, her crimson and gold leather armor was half buckled, and her wooden sword bounced on her back. The sword had a crack in it now, from Midsummer. She still called it Justice.

“You didn’t wait!” she said.

“It isn’t your turn.”

“It’s never my turn. The Steward thinks I’ll ring it too hard.” She fell in beside him, puffing like a horse after a gallop. “You can’t ring a bell too hard.”

“You rang it eleven times.”

“That was practice.”

Farther up the hill, somebody very large was apologizing.

“Sorry. Sorry, I didn’t see you there. You’re in the road, though, which isn’t really— No. No, that’s fine. You stay. I’ll go around.”

Alfred Thatch went around. He was twelve, very tall and very broad, with warm brown skin, curly black hair and boots like two small boats. The sheep in the road watched him go around with the calm of a sheep that has never once been asked to move. Alfie’s green tunic was covered in wool. His family kept the sheep of the Sheepwalk, and every banner in Pebblecrown started as Thatch wool. That meant every banner in Pebblecrown started with Alfie saying sorry to something.
