The Yokai Knows Your Name

A Spine-Chilling Japanese Folklore Horror Novel for Ages 10-14

by Selma Ravensby

Ages 10–14Level 3Spooky
KindlePaperback

On the shortest day of the year, Sumi Okada hears her little sister call her name from the dark mountain road. Once. But Koto is at home in the bath.

In the mountain village of Yugure, every child knows the rule: never answer the first call at dusk. A real person always calls twice.

The next morning, Koto cannot sing. On the second dusk, Koto hears Sumi's voice calling from the terraces, and she answers. At last light she is gone, and the village begins to forget she was ever born. Her name fades from her school cubby. Even Mom asks, "Who?"

Only Sumi, her loud best friend Teppei, and Wataru, the new boy who records everything, still remember. They have until the last light of Christmas Eve to climb to the empty Upper School, where something has waited twelve winters for someone to call, and to find out what it truly needs while it hunts them with their own voices.

A genuinely creepy, never gory novel for readers 10 to 14, built on traditional Japanese folklore told with care: short chapters, real suspense, a quiet heroine who finds her voice, and an Author's Note on what is traditional and what is invented. For fans of The Mimic, the Roblox horror experience, and of haunted villages at dusk.

For parents

Format
Tween thriller: 20 to 26 chapters, written for about a 4th to 6th grade reading level.
How scary
Scary, for brave readers 10 and up: dread that builds, no gore, and nobody dies on the page.
At the back of the book
An author's note that explains which parts are traditional folklore and which are invented, plus a note for grown-ups at the front.

A good fit for kids who are 10 or older and love ghost stories, folklore and Japan.
