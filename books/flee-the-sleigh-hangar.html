Read the opening pages

Crack the terminals. Dodge the Chiller. Go back for your friends before the frost reaches the top.

A Christmas update drops a brand-new map: the Sleigh Hangar, with a giant red sleigh and a row of frost pods that glow cold blue. At midnight on Christmas Eve, it disappears.

Four friends from the same apartment building have four nights to pull off their first Full Thaw, where every one of them escapes. Anika plans the routes. Grace cracks the terminals and has never made a red flag. Wilder lures the Chiller away with loud, silly loops. And eight-year-old Tolu is new to the crew, so her big sister keeps telling her to hide.

Then ColdFront, the most famous Chiller anywhere, picks Snowfall: anyone who isn't hiding glows right through the walls. Friends get frosted and trapped in pods, and the clock keeps running. To win, Grace will have to miss on purpose and trust her little sister with the scariest job in the game.

Read the opening pages

The plank over the Furnace Room door said NOBODY STAYS IN THE POD in purple paint. The letters got smaller toward the end, because Tolu had run out of plank.

Grace Adeyinka had walked under that sign a hundred times. Tonight she stopped and looked at it, and then she looked at her little sister.

Tolu was sitting on the saggy couch with her purple backpack still on. She was holding a laptop on her knees as if it were a birthday cake.

“Good as new,” Mr. Pappas said, and he patted the lid. Four strips of silver tape held one corner together. “Well, good as old. It turns on, and that is the main thing.”

“It’s perfect,” Tolu said in her tiny voice.

Mr. Pappas was the super of the Ironwood, the old brick apartment building where all four of them lived. He had a big gray mustache, reading glasses pushed up on his head, and a toolbelt that jingled when he laughed. He had cleared out this basement room next to the boiler and given it to them. It held two saggy couches, a long folding table, and a ping-pong table with one leg on a stack of phone books.

And now there were four laptops on the table instead of three.

“An early Christmas present,” Mr. Pappas said. “From me and my tape.”

The old boiler hummed in the corner, and snow was piling up against the little window near the ceiling. It was the first night of winter break, and the Furnace Room was as warm as a pocket.

“The update drops at four,” said Anika Desai, checking her big plastic wristwatch. Her black ponytail swung. “It’s three fifty-eight.”

“A Christmas map,” said Wilder Haskins. His knee was bouncing so hard that the folding table shook. His red hair stuck straight up in front, as if it were excited too. “Christmas, people! No homework for two whole weeks. Just snow and Roblox and—”

“Chores,” said Anika.

“Snow and Roblox and chores,” Wilder agreed.

Grace drummed her fingers on the table. She drummed them on every table she ever sat at, and she couldn’t help it.

“She’s eight,” Grace said.

“Eight and a half,” said Tolu.

“The Chiller doesn’t care about the half.”

Nobody said anything for a second, and the old boiler hummed in the quiet.

Grace knew what the Chiller was better than anyone. It was the tall white shape that hunted you in every round of the game. It had a lamp on its helmet and a long pole in its hand. One touch from the glowing tip of that pole and you were frosted stiff. Then it slid you into a glass frost pod, and you stayed there until a teammate came back for you.
