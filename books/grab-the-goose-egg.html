Grab the Goose Egg

A Funny Gamer-Squad Adventure for Kids 8-12

Read the opening pages

Sneak past the giant goose. Grab the egg. RUN.

Ten-year-old Zeke Delgado is the fastest runner on his gaming squad, the Speedy Yolks, and the loudest. There's one problem. In the whole history of the team, they have pulled off exactly zero big steals.

Then a giant, glowing egg appears in Duchess the goose's nest. It's the rarest egg on the server. It's also so heavy that nobody can outrun the goose while carrying it.

Zeke's big sister Abril makes plan after plan. His best friend Kiyo crunches every number. Abuela Toña, a retired umpire with the slowest speed on the server, calls the plays from the kitchen table. And a show-off rival named MegaYoink offers Zeke a secret glitch that could get the egg home in nine seconds.

Zeke wants the win more than anything. But the kitchen timer is ticking, and the only fair way to get that egg home means doing the one thing a runner hates most: letting go.

Read the opening pages

IN the whole history of the Speedy Yolks, we had pulled off exactly zero big steals.

Not zero this week, and not zero since lunch. Zero, ever.

A big steal is any egg better than Common that makes it all the way home to your pen. We had stolen about a hundred Commons. Anything shinier than that, we had never once gotten home.

“In baseball,” Abuela said, “we call that a goose egg.”

She was standing at the fridge with a marker, drawing a big round zero on the Plan Board. Then she gave it a beak and two skinny feet.

“Abuela,” said Abril, “that’s my board.”

“It’s a goose egg, mija,” said Abuela. “It needed a goose.”

“Can you at least draw it in green? Green is for ideas.”

“This isn’t an idea,” said Abuela. “This is the score.”

That was Monday at 4:30 in the afternoon, at Mission Control. Mission Control is our kitchen table, and our team is the Speedy Yolks. Our scoreboard is a goose with legs.

Here’s the squad, in the order they sat down.

Abril sat down first, because Abril is always first. She’s twelve, with a long braid, purple glasses and a dry-erase marker behind her ear at all times. She color-codes everything, even her socks.

“Mission Control is now in session,” she said, lining up her laptop with the edge of the table.

Kiyo came in the back door without knocking, because he lives next door and knocking takes too long. He’s ten like me, but taller, with hair like a mop that gave up. His hoodie pocket is full of index cards, one for every pet he has ever seen in the game.

“Did you know Crouton earned 2,700 coins on Saturday?” he said.

“Kiyo,” I said, “I always know.”

Abuela sat down last, slowly, with her tablet and her reading glasses on a beaded chain. She was an umpire for kids’ baseball for thirty years, and she still has the voice. She has short silver curls, a little baseball pin on her cardigan, and a pickle. Nobody knows where she gets the pickles.

“Is everybody ready to get honked?” she asked.

“Abuela,” I said, “today we’re not getting honked. Today we’re winning.”

“That’s what you said yesterday,” said Kiyo.

“Yesterday was Sunday. We didn’t even play.”

“That’s what you said last Monday, then,” said Kiyo.

And me? I’m Zeke, the runner. I have curly black hair, a gap in my front teeth, and exactly one sock on. Nobody knows where the other sock went.

Mom came in and picked up the Tomato.

The Tomato is a kitchen timer shaped like a tomato. It lives on the counter, right under the Squad Code, which is taped to the fridge in Abuela’s handwriting. Rule five is underlined twice, because it’s Mom’s rule.
