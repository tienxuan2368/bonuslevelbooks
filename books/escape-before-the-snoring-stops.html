Read the opening pages

Shh. Don't wake the dough.

It's a stormy night at the Bouncy Crust, and the heat is turned up to TOASTY. That's bad news, because a giant ball of pizza dough is asleep in the Cool Bowl, and warm wakes him up.

His name is Kneady. The K is silent. Tonight, you need Kneady to be silent too.

He has oozed out in his sleep, kept Mo's rain boots, and filled the Party Room wall to wall. The Safety Lock has shut every door, and the only grown-up is outside chasing her umbrella. To reach the Exit, you have to tiptoe over him with Kip (who always wiggles too soon), Nia (who counts every snore) and Mo (who is not little, he is QUIET-sized). As long as Kneady snores, he sleeps.

There's just one problem. Every snore puffs flour into the air. And Mo has the biggest sneeze in Jumpington.

Fall, and you go BLOOP back to your last flag. Then you try again.

Can you escape before the snoring stops?

START [1] - - - - - - - - - EXIT

Plip. Plop. PLOP.

Rain drums on the round windows of the Bouncy Crust. The wind whooshes up the hill. Out on the sidewalk, every hop-tile is a puddle.

You live in a town where every building is a course. The bus stop has a slide. The park has bounce-benches. And the best course in town is a pizza shop called the Bouncy Crust.

It’s almost closing time. You are wet from your hat to your toes.

The town is called Jumpington. Tonight it should be called Splashington.

“Easy-peasy, rainy-squeezy!” says a kid in a backwards red cap. That’s Kip. He has freckles, one green sweatband and sneakers with yellow lightning stripes. Rain drips off his cap onto his nose.

“That doesn’t rhyme,” says Nia. She wipes her big round purple glasses on her orange overalls. Her ponytail looks like a wet paintbrush. A pencil sits behind her ear, like always.

squelch-squeak. squelch-squeak.

That’s Mo, Nia’s little brother. He wears a blue pom-pom hat and red rain boots. His boots always squeak, and tonight they squelch, too.

“I’m not little,” says Mo. “I’m FAST-sized. But my boots are SWIMMING-sized.”

“My little meatballs!” A tall, skinny lady hurries over. Her white hair is a cloud with a pencil and a whisk stuck in it. Swim goggles sit on her forehead. Her lab coat has ten pockets and a lot of flour. That’s Ms. Doughwhistle. She owns the Bouncy Crust, and she invented almost everything in it.

“Your grown-ups called,” she says. “The rain is so big, they’ll be late. Coats and boots on the warm vent, please!”

She leads you down the back hallway. Tall stacks of party chairs line the walls, ready for a party tomorrow. At the end is the Exit hall, by the big green Exit Door. Coat hooks hang over a warm, humming vent.

Next to the vent sits a big steel bowl with a lid. The Cool Bowl. It’s frosty all over, like a glass of cold lemonade.

You know what’s inside. Everybody on Team Checkpoint knows.

From under the lid comes a tiny sound.

Mmmf… mmmf…

“Shh,” whispers Ms. Doughwhistle. “He’s snoring. As long as he snores, he sleeps.”

Inside the bowl sleeps Kneady. He’s a giant ball of pizza dough who came alive by accident. Warm wakes him up, and cold puts him to sleep. Right now, he’s cold and small and snoring.

Mo pulls off his boots and pours out the rain. He sets them on the warm vent, right next to the Cool Bowl.

“Bye, boots,” he says. He wiggles his toes in his socks with red and blue stripes.

Ms. Doughwhistle turns a dial on the wall. One end says LOW, and the other end says TOASTY. She turns it all the way to TOASTY.
