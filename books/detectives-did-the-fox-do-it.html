Read the opening pages

The whole town says the fox did it. The detectives say: prove it.

On the morning before Founders' Day, the treasure of Riverlatch is gone. The Founders' Heart has vanished from a locked hall in the night, and the stone pressure plate by the only door never clicked once. By sunrise, everyone blames Pockets, the fox who naps under Latch Bridge. Then a trail of redstone dust leads straight to the clubhouse of the Redstone Detectives, and Jory, Imani and Wynn become the suspects.

Shut out of the case, the club runs its first night stakeout in the rain. Drowned climb out of the river. A trident slams into the shutters. The lamps on the Old Line go dark while Wynn is still out on the tracks. To clear their names before sunset, the club will need every clue, every test and a lot of courage.

Before the big reveal, the book stops and asks: CAN YOU SOLVE IT?

Every clue in this suspenseful solve-it-yourself mystery works the way it does in Minecraft, so a careful reader can beat the detectives to the answer. Short chapters, a fair-play puzzle, a one-horned goat, real danger from night mobs and no gore. Case Solutions and a Detective Skills Quiz wait at the back. It's a story about checking before you blame.

Read the opening pages

This is the case file for the Founders’ Heart. I am writing it down so that nobody in Riverlatch ever says we took it, not even as a joke.

Case note: the Constable’s whistle went off at sunrise on Founders’ Eve. It was three long blasts, and in Riverlatch, three blasts mean one thing. Come to the bell, now.

I was at the Post Office, sorting parcels for Mom. I grabbed the Case Book and ran.

It was raining, the way it almost always is here. The rain fell in long straight streaks from square gray clouds, and the sun was a pale square low over the river.

Bell Square was filling up fast. The Constable had already rung the bell, so every villager on the square had hurried home to bed. That is what a bell does to villagers, even in the morning.

Constable Gert Tolliver stood in the doorway of the Town Hall. Her big boots were planted on the stone pressure plate just inside, so the iron door stayed open behind her.

“Nobody touch anything!” she boomed. “By the book!”

She pointed over her shoulder. On the back wall, under a new red banner, hung an item frame.

It was empty.

For a hundred years, that frame had held the Founders’ Heart. It is a Heart of the Sea, round and deep blue, with a glow inside it like a lamp under water. Tomorrow at sunset, it was supposed to be carried across Latch Bridge for Founders’ Day.

Now it was gone.

“Gone,” said Imani Brooks, stopping beside me. Her satchel clinked when she stopped. It always does. She keeps repeaters in it, and comparators, and one spare stone pressure plate, the way other kids keep snacks.

“Gone since when?” I asked.

Imani nodded at the Constable’s office across the square, where a redstone lamp glowed orange beside the window. “That’s the Watch Lamp,” she said. “I built it for her yesterday. It’s lit now.”

I opened the Case Book and wrote that down. I write everything down, because that is the first job of a detective. Guessing is not on the list at all.

Something yellow shot across the square. It was Wynn Tolliver, in her dyed leather boots, with her berry pouch bouncing.

“Did you know a bell sends villagers to bed even in the morning?” she panted. “Mom rang it, and the Librarian ran so fast his robe flapped. Is the Heart really gone? Who took it? Do we have a suspect? Can I interview somebody?”

“Yes,” I said. “Nobody knows. No. Not yet.”

“You always say not yet.”

“Because it’s always not yet, at the start.”

Then a voice rang out from the Rumor Board. “BREAKING!”
