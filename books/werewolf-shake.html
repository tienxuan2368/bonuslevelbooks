Read the opening pages

It's the night of the full moon, and the moon isn't coming out.

Shep Howell is eleven, a werewolf, and really bad at holding still. Every full moon, his whole family turns into wolves and runs in the deep spruce woods. But this time the rain hides the moon, and the Turn gets stuck halfway. Ears. Tail. Nose. No wolf. All night long.

And it's all happening at his best friend Hana's sleepover, in front of two friends who can't find out.

Then, at midnight, twenty zombies walk out of the dark corners of Mossrock. Some of them don't wait at the door. One is small enough to squeeze through a hole one block big. Spiders are climbing the walls, the armadillo who keeps them away has rolled into a ball, and when the sun finally comes up, nothing burns.

With no wolves and no grown-ups, the pack has to follow the one member who never turns at all: Hana, the girl with the crossbow and the plan.

Read the opening pages

The first thing my ears catch on Ears Day is my best friend planning a party without me.

I’m in the library hall, which is also our school. Mr. Pettibone is hanging spelling words on the board, one little sign at a time. Out in the square, a bucket bumps against the well. Two rooms away, the Librarian turns a page.

I hear all of it. That’s what three nights to full moon does to a Howell.

Pause.

I’m Shep Howell, and I’m eleven, and my family is a pack of werewolves. It isn’t a curse, and nobody got bitten. It runs in Mom’s family, the way curly hair runs in some families. Three nights before every full moon, our ears go first.

Okay. Back to the party.

“Saturday,” Hana whispers. She’s in the back row with Brynn Dalgaard, and she thinks nobody can hear her. “At the Trading Post. Pie, blankets, the loft. You and Gil.”

My hair is standing up in two pointy tufts on top of my head, like ears. That’s because they basically are. They swivel toward the back row all by themselves.

Saturday is the full moon.

“No offense,” Brynn whispers, “but is Shep coming? The Howells are never around on full moons.”

“They’re busy that night,” Hana says.

She says it the way you’d say the sky is blue. Easy. Almost happy.

I stare hard at the spelling sign. FORTUNATELY. I don’t feel fortunate at all.

Hana Mori has been my best friend since we were six. She has two long black braids tied with blue ribbons and a leather satchel full of plans. There’s a brush in her belt for digging up old treasure. She knows everything about my family. Everything.

And she’s having a sleepover on the one night I can’t come.

° • °

“Shep!” Gil Bixby leans over from the next bench, and his pockets clack. Gil is nine, and he fishes bones out of the creek. “Did you hear? Full moon on Saturday! Big Pale will be OUT!”

Big Pale is the giant pale wolf that Gil has been trying to tame for a year. Gil doesn’t know that Big Pale is me. Nobody knows, except my family and Hana.

“Cool,” I say.

“I’m going to look for him out the loft window,” Gil says. “Hana said I can bring my fishing rod. You never know when the creek is biting.”

Hana said. Of course she did.

Up front, Mr. Pettibone, our teacher, lifts his goat horn. He ends every school day with it.

Bwaaa!

On a normal day it’s loud. On Ears Day it’s like being inside the horn. I clap my hands over my head, which looks strange, because my hands aren’t where normal ears go.
