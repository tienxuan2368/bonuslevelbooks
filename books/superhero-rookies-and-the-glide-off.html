Read the opening pages

Somebody swapped the star racer's rockets. Now the Fizzles are the only heroes close enough to bring him home.

At Blockburst Academy, every student's superpower works exactly like something real in the blocky world. Rafi Okafor's power is wind: four puffs, and then he needs a snack. His squad, the Fizzles, is ranked dead last. So at the Glide-Off, a sky race around Cloudcap Peak, they get the worst job on the mountain: crewing for Beckett Rowe, the show-off flier who gave them their nickname.

Then the rockets in Beckett's barrel turn out to be duds. Beckett comes up short, flies too close to a pillager patrol and crash-lands in powder snow under Grimtooth Ridge at sunset. He can't climb out. The crossbows on the rocks above him are loaded. And Frankie, the fastest Fizzle, is one hill away from winning the first race of her life.

To bring Beckett home, the Fizzles will need four puffs of wind, twenty tough seconds, one floating hat, a glowing allay, a pair of borrowed boots and every rule of the snow.

Read the opening pages

Coach Upton had built the new Gear rack himself, out of spruce planks and a great deal of shouting. It held twenty-four shields and twenty-four wooden training swords, and a brand-new sign hung over the top.

SIGN OUT ONE.

SIGN IN ONE.

COUNTING IS

A HERO SKILL.

“Shield first!” Coach boomed. “Sword second! Plan always! HUSTLE!”

He was huge and red-bearded, with a goat horn on a cord around his neck. He strode toward the doorway to blow it. The doorway was a normal size. Coach Upton was not.

bonk

“I’m fine!” he shouted from the floor. “Doorframes build character!”

Rafi Okafor signed out one shield and one wooden sword. The shield was heavier than it looked, and the sword was lighter, and neither one looked like it belonged on a poster.

“Why do first-years need Gear all of a sudden?” asked Wendell Tran. He was tall and broad, with round glasses and a turtle shell for a helmet.

“Because of the patrol,” said Rafi.

Last week, from the top of the Rail, his dad had seen four grey pillagers walking the Snowy Slopes above the academy. They had come down from the outpost on Grimtooth Ridge, lower than anyone could remember. One of them carried a dark banner high above its head. Coach had built the rack the very next morning.

+ * +

The Pistonworks was the academy’s training hall, full of pistons, slime pads, sticky honey floors and dispensers that spat snowballs. Today every dispenser on the wall was pointed at the middle of the room.

“Snowball drill!” Coach bellowed. “Shields up! Get to the far wall! Squad Six, you’re first!”

Squad Six was the Fizzles. On their very first day they had fizzled so badly that they landed sixth of six on the Hero Board. The name stuck. They liked it that way.

The dispensers clicked.

Frankie Ramirez went first, because Frankie always went first. Bright cyan swirls rose off her, and she was across the room before the snowballs were. Three of them thumped her shield. She bit a strip of dried kelp in half at the far wall.

“Four seconds,” she said. “Next.”

Gigi Larsen faded out. Her whole body went invisible, but her tall magenta cap did not. Neither did her training sword. They floated across the room together, with near-white swirls drifting around them.

“A floating sword is less comforting than you’d think,” said Wendell.

“I’m not a floating sword,” said the cap. “I’m a mystery.”

Then Wendell switched on his Shell. Blue-grey and violet swirls rose off him, and he went very slow and very tough. The snowballs bounced off him like rain off a roof.

“One cobble,” he said calmly. “Two cobble.”
