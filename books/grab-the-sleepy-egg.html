Read the opening pages

The rarest egg on the server only grows at night. And night is when the Nabbers come.

Ten-year-old Zeke Delgado and his gaming squad, the Speedy Yolks, finally grab a Secret egg from the top of a volcano, right out from under a giant glowing salamander. It's called the Sleepy Egg, and it needs three whole nights to hatch. That's three nights of guarding it from the Night Nabbers, a silent rival crew who walk into pens and carry off any egg that hasn't hatched yet.

Then the first falling stars come down, and everybody runs to catch one. Everybody except Abuela Toña, a retired umpire and the slowest player on the server. She stays home to guard the egg. And three raiders walk right past her.

To steal it back before it hatches as somebody else's pet, Abuela will have to do the one thing she swore she'd never do: step on the hamster wheel.

EGGS: 0 — GROW: 3 NIGHTS

MY grandmother has said “I am not a hamster” one hundred and eleven times since we started playing. I kept score.

Keep that number in your head. Before this replay is over, you are going to see her on a hamster wheel. Walking. It’s on the tape. I’ve watched it nine times.

But first, the egg that snores.

The wind blew all week. Not fun wind. The kind that rattles the kitchen window and rolls the trash cans down the street by themselves.

On Thursday, Abuela sat at our kitchen table with her reading glasses on their beaded chain and a pickle in her hand. She was an umpire for kids’ baseball for thirty years. She can still hear a foul ball from three fields away.

“Abuela,” I said. “The speed sign at Mount Toasty says sixty thousand. I’m sixty thousand two hundred twelve. Abril’s sixty thousand forty. Kiyo’s sixty-one thousand.”

“Congratulations,” said Abuela. She crunched her pickle.

“Your speed is fifty-one.”

“I know my speed, mijo.”

“You could get on the treadmill for, like, one day.”

“I am not a hamster,” said Abuela.

SCOREBOARD: NOT A HAMSTER 112, HAMSTER WHEEL 0.

Quick introductions, before the Tomato starts.

Me: Zeke Delgado, ten. Curly hair, a gap in my front teeth, and one sock, because the other one is always somewhere else. I’m the runner on our team, the Speedy Yolks.

My big sister, Abril, is twelve. Long braid, purple glasses, a dry-erase marker behind one ear. She plans everything. Her new binder said Toasty on the cover, in orange marker, with flames drawn around it.

My best friend, Kiyo, lives next door and knows every number in the world. He was sorting his index cards next to his laptop. He has one card for every pet on our server.

Then Mom picked up the Tomato.

The Tomato is our kitchen timer. It’s red and round and shaped like a tomato, and it is the boss of us. On Thursdays it gets sixty minutes. Mom twisted it all the way around.

Tick, tick, tick.

We put on our headphones, and the kitchen went away.

Base Row popped up around me. Seven bases in a row, one for every player on our server. Every base has a pen for your pets, a treadmill for training and a colorful banner flapping on a tall pole.

In my pen, my pigeon Crouton cooed and earned his one coin a second. He is the best pigeon on the server. Nobody has ever argued with me about it, probably because nobody else wants to argue about pigeons.

Abuela’s base is the first one on the row. She calls it home plate. Her avatar, SafeOrOut, is a tiny blocky umpire in navy blue, with her mask pushed up on her silver curls. She walked to the front of her pen and crossed her arms.
