A Creepy-Funny Family Chapter Book for Kids 8-12

Read the opening pages

Grandmother Ottilie has never had a birthday. Not in four hundred years. This year, her family is throwing her a surprise party anyway.

Eleven-year-old Mina Nightshade and her mom have it all planned: four cakes (in Hollowbrook, a cake only holds one candle), a secret party at Mom's night bakery while the village sleeps, and a great-aunt who has walked here from the mountains. Then her little brother Barney toasts the birthday girl with some "fancy juice" he found on a battlefield.

It isn't juice. It's an ominous bottle.

At midnight, the village bell starts to ring, and villagers ring it for one thing only. A horn sounds in the east. Wave after wave of raiders pours into sleeping Hollowbrook: pillagers with crossbows, axe-swinging vindicators, giggling witches, a charging ravager and an evoker whose magic makes the ground bite. When the sun comes up, the raiders don't burn, and the sun takes the Nightshades' strength instead.

To save the village, Mina will need a shield, a pocket full of snowballs, a nosy neighbor with a spyglass, and the one person who has spent four hundred years hiding from being seen.

A creepy, funny family adventure set in the blocky world of Minecraft, with real game mechanics, a seven-wave raid and one very useful pumpkin. For grown-ups: short chapters, easy reading, real shivers with no gore, and a warm story about being proud of who you are.

Read the opening pages

By the end of Grandmother’s four hundredth birthday, our village had been attacked seven times.

Every attack was Barney’s fault.

To be fair to Barney, he was only trying to be kind. That is how most bad nights start.

It began on a Wednesday, with a word nobody in my house is allowed to say.

“Birthday,” Mom whispered.

She said it in the kitchen, with the door shut. She held a tray of cookies up like a shield. Dad was polishing his crossbow by the window. I was doing my homework upside down on a chair. It annoys Barney, who can’t do it yet.

“Shh,” said Dad. “She’ll hear you.”

“She’s up in the moon-window room. She can’t hear a thing from up there.”

Above us, something screeched across the roof. The phantoms were back. They come for my family every night. Phantoms hunt anyone who hasn’t slept for three nights. My family hasn’t slept at night in four hundred years.

Pudding sat on the windowsill with her tail lashing. She’s our cat: all black, orange eyes, and the one thing phantoms are afraid of. She hissed at the ceiling, once, to remind them.

I should tell you who we are.

I’m Mina Nightshade. I’m eleven. I have a black bob with one white streak, red-violet eyes and a short black cape with a crimson lining. My family is night folk. We nap in armchairs, we sneeze smoke at sunrise, and we are very bad at being normal.

And my grandmother is very bad at birthdays.

“She’s turning four hundred,” Mom said. “Four hundred, Augustin. Somebody has to do something.”

“She doesn’t have birthdays,” said Dad. “She told me so on the night I married you. I quote: ‘I do not have birthdays. I have anniversaries of being difficult to get rid of.’”

That sounded exactly like her.

Grandmother Ottilie is tall and silver-haired, with a bun shaped like a tower. Her cane has a purple amethyst on top. She has watched four hundred years of sunrises from behind a curtain. She doesn’t like fuss. She doesn’t like cake with writing on it. Most of all, she doesn’t like being counted.

“Night folk are never counted, child,” she told me once. “A thing that is counted can be found.”

Mom unfolded one of Grandmother’s moon charts from her apron. One circle had a floury fingerprint on it.

“She was born on a night with a very full moon,” Mom said. “The next full moon is Saturday.”

“Three nights from now,” I said.

“A surprise party. At Midnight Crumbs, after the baking’s done. The whole village will be asleep. Nobody will see a thing.”

Midnight Crumbs is Mom’s bakery on the village square. It’s open all night, which is the most normal thing about my family.
