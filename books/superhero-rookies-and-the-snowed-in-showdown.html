Read the opening pages

Three nights. No Rail. One hero who won't go to bed.

At Blockburst Academy, every student's superpower works exactly like something real in the blocky world. Rafi Okafor's power is wind: four puffs, and then he needs a snack. His squad, the Fizzles, is ranked fifth of six. Then, in the first big snowstorm of the year, a creeper blows a hole in the Rail on the night ten little kids from town came up to sleep over.

Now the academy is cut off. Zombies hammer at the wooden doors. Skeletons wander into the powder snow and climb out as strays, with arrows that make you slow. And Gigi Larsen, who can turn invisible except for her hat, has decided that real heroes never sleep.

She is wrong. On the third night, something comes out of the sky to prove it. And seven-year-old Lenny has gone up the mountain alone to find his cat.

To bring everyone home, the Fizzles will need snow golems, carved pumpkins, one stubborn calico cat and the bravest move in the whole book.

Read the opening pages

NIGHT TONIGHT.

SNOW COMING.

WEAR LEATHER.

The first cart came up the Rail on Friday afternoon. It was packed with ten very small kids. It was also packed with a very large amount of noise.

“FIZZLES! FIZZLES! FIZZLES!”

That was Lenny Nakamura. He was seven. He was Rafi Okafor’s biggest fan. He had been chanting since the bottom of the mountain. His little sister Bitsy sat beside him. Her pigtails were stuffed under a little leather cap, and she had a cookie in each hand.

Once a year, on Little Heroes Night, the little kids of Stoneskip rode up to watch the first-years show off their Bursts. Then they slept in the Great Hall in a row of red beds, like a real hero squad.

Something small and soft landed in the empty cart behind him with a pop. It was a cat, white and orange and black in patches, with four white paws. A name tag hung from her collar.

“Mittens!” Lenny scooped her up. “She always finds me.”

Here is a fact about tame cats. If you walk more than twelve blocks away from yours, it doesn’t bother walking after you. It just pops up right beside you.

Mittens had popped up three times on the way. She did not look tired. She looked smug.

+ * +

The show was in the Pistonworks, the academy’s training hall.

Rafi went first. He was ten, with short black twists that always blew backward, a sky-blue tunic and a long orange scarf. His Burst was Gust. It worked exactly like a wind charge, and it came in fours. Four puffs, and then he was empty.

“Steady,” he told his hands.

fwoomp

The puff shot across the hall and rang the big bell on the far wall. Ten little kids screamed like he had saved the world.

Frankie Ramirez went next. Her Burst was Swift. Cyan swirls lifted off her lime-green tunic, and she ran three laps of the hall before anybody could count to three. Then she stopped dead and ate a strip of dried kelp in under a second.

“Hungry heroes can’t sprint,” she told the front row. “Bring snacks. Always.”

Wendell Tran stepped up. He was eleven, tall and broad, with round glasses and a turtle shell on his head. His Burst was Shell. Blue-grey and violet swirls rose off him, and for twenty seconds he turned very slow and very, very tough. The dispensers pelted him with snowballs. He didn’t even blink.

“One cobble,” he counted calmly. “Two cobble. Three cobble…”

Then it was Gigi’s turn.

+ * +

Gigi Larsen did not walk onto a stage. She arrived.

“ENTER GIGI, STAGE LEFT!”

She had a red braid, a hundred freckles and a purple tunic. She also had a tall magenta leather cap that she never, ever took off. Her Burst was Fade. It worked exactly like Invisibility.
