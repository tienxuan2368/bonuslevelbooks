Read the opening pages

The dough is awake. And it wants to KEEP you.

It's your very first day as a runner at the Bouncy Crust, the bounciest course in Jumpington. The floor is sauce. The pizza boxes float. And at closing time, one spoonful too much Bounce Yeast wakes up the giant Pizza Day dough.

His name is Kneady. The K is silent. Kneady is not.

The Safety Lock shuts every door, and the only grown-up is stuck outside. Now you have to run the whole course, checkpoint by checkpoint, with Kip (who always jumps first), Nia (who waits for it... waits for it... NOW!) and Mo (who is not little, he is FAST-sized). The whole building is warming up for Pizza Day, so Kneady gets bigger every minute. He follows anything warm. He oozes under doors. And whatever he grabs, he keeps.

When you fall, you go BLOOP and pop back at your last flag. Then you try again.

Can you escape before the dough rises?

Read the opening pages

Ready?

Then put one sneaker on the glowing green pad by the door. Now put the other one up there, too.

ding!

The little checkered flag in the middle flips over. Just like that, it knows you. That’s what checkpoint flags do. They remember runners.

This is your very first checkpoint. Today is your very first day as a runner. You’re at the bounciest course in Jumpington.

In Jumpington, every building is a course. The sidewalks have hop-tiles. The bus stop has a slide. Everybody has square shoulders and springy legs. They can jump higher than their own heads! The best course of all sits on Pepper Hill. It’s a pizzeria called the Bouncy Crust.

“Welcome to the Sauce Hall!” yells a kid in a backwards red cap. That’s Kip. He has freckles, one green sweatband and sneakers with yellow lightning stripes. “Easy-peasy, pizza-squeezy!”

“It is NOT easy,” says a girl in big purple glasses. She has a pencil behind her ear. That’s Nia. “Rule number one. Don’t touch the red.”

The floor of the Sauce Hall is red and shiny, because it is SAUCE. It’s cool, sticky pizza sauce, from wall to wall. Pizza boxes float across it like tiny rafts on a river.

Red means BLOOP. Everybody in Jumpington knows that.

“Is it a big jump?” asks the smallest runner. That’s Mo, Nia’s little brother. He has a blue pom-pom hat and red rain boots that go squeak-squeak. “Is it bigger than me? Because I’m not little. I’m FAST-sized!”

You look down at your sneakers. Your left shoelace is flopping loose. It always is.

A box floats close. You jump. Your shoelace snags on the pad, and you wobble, and you flap.

SPLOOSH!

BLOOP!

You pop right back up on the flag, safe and totally fine. There’s flour on your nose. Why flour? Nobody knows. It’s always flour.

“BOUNCE BACK!” the whole team yells.

Nobody laughs at you. Kip whoops, but it’s a cheer.

“Try two,” says Nia. She taps the beat on her glasses with one finger. “Wait for it… wait for it… NOW!”

You jump, but the box isn’t there yet. You land in the sauce.

BLOOP!

You land right back on the flag. Now there’s sauce in your ear, too.

Nia’s mouth makes a little O. Then she giggles. “Oops. That was a NOT-YET.”

Across the hall, a boy with gold sneakers and very neat hair waves at you. That’s Lance. “Watch and learn, slowpokes. Shortcut!”

Lance leaps for the corner. The corner is red. He goes in with a splash so big that it rains sauce. He pops up on his flag, and his neat hair isn’t neat anymore.

“That,” says Lance, “was a warm-up.” Then his dad calls from the door, and he hops home.
