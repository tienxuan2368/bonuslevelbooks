Read the opening pages

One giant rock grump. One glowing stone. One kid who freezes every time it's his turn.

Every half hour, a crack opens in the sky over Grumble Hill, and Rumbletum climbs out: a mossy boss the size of a hill, with a huge boss egg riding on his back. He stomps, he charges, he rains rocks, and he knocks the whole server flat.

Ten-year-old Zeke Delgado and his gaming squad, the Speedy Yolks, have never beaten him. To win, someone has to lead his glowing Grumble Stone into the Bell Posts three times. And the Stone keeps picking Zeke's best friend, Kiyo, who knows every number in the game but freezes the second everyone is watching. Then the kitchen timer dings right in the middle of the fight.

With a rival who always bonks the most, a silent server legend, a brand-new player and a grandma umpire with the slowest speed on the server, the squad gets one last try before the boss egg is gone.

Read the opening pages

EGGS: 0 — WOBBLE: 10:00

ROLL the tape. Freeze it right there.

That’s my best friend, Kiyo Tanaka. Well, that’s his avatar, PocketWiki. Green beanie, giant round headphones, one index card sticking out of his pocket. He’s standing in the middle of a ring of huge gray stones, and he isn’t moving.

That orange glowing thing flying at him is a stone the size of a beach ball. Over his head, his name is flashing red.

Everybody at our kitchen table is yelling the same word. “Go!”

Kiyo isn’t going.

Okay. Rewind. To understand how we got here, you need to know about the egg report.

It rained the whole week. Not fun rain with puddles to stomp in. The gray kind, where the kitchen window looks like somebody is running a hose over it all day.

On Thursday, Kiyo stood in front of our fridge holding five index cards. He lives next door, so he’s basically at our house more than our house is.

“Okay,” he said. “So.”

That was it. That was the whole report.

“Keep going, mijo,” said Abuela. She was at the table with her reading glasses on their beaded chain and a pickle in her hand. She was an umpire for kids’ baseball for thirty years. She can tell when somebody is about to strike out.

Kiyo looked at his first card. “The kiwi,” he said, “is a bird that lives in…”

His mouth stayed open. Nothing else came out.

Here’s the thing about Kiyo. He knows every number in the world. How many eggs a bird lays. How long they take to hatch. How fast every guardian in our game can run. He tells me numbers all day long, at recess, on the bus, through the fence.

But on Monday, he has to tell them to our whole class, for five minutes, out loud, standing up. The report is called “Eggs by the Numbers,” and he has been freezing on the word “kiwi” since Sunday.

“That counts as practice,” said Mom. She patted his shoulder. “Sit. Homework’s done.”

Kiyo sat down like his legs were relieved.

Then Mom picked up the Tomato. That’s our kitchen timer. It’s red and round and shaped like a tomato, and it is the boss of us. On Thursdays, it gets sixty minutes.

She twisted it all the way around, and it started to tick.

Here’s the rest of the squad, fast, because the Tomato doesn’t wait.

Me: Zeke Delgado, ten years old, with curly hair and a gap in my front teeth. I wear one sock, because the other one is always somewhere else. I’m the runner for our team, the Speedy Yolks.

My big sister, Abril, is twelve, with a long braid, purple glasses and a dry-erase marker behind one ear. She plans everything. She had a brand-new binder in front of her, and on the cover, in purple marker, it said BOSS NIGHT.
