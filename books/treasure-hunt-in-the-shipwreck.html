Read the opening pages

Captain Dylan Lockett has a crew, a flag, a parrot who outranks him, and a ship that has never moved an inch.

So when a dolphin leads his little fleet of boats to a shipwreck lying on its side, and his shipwright swims up with a real treasure map, Dylan knows this is it: the find that will ring the bell on Harbor Day. There is just one problem. Captain Cordelia Prowse, the fanciest captain in the bay, saw the map too.

The race is on, across the shallows and out over the deep, dark water, until the sun goes down and the drowned rise with their tridents. When Cordelia's boat is smashed and she is stranded on a sandbar, Dylan has to choose between turning for home and going back through the monsters for his rival. And the treasure? His quiet little sister has been trying to tell him where it is all along.

This swashbuckling, funny, action-packed voyage sails the oceans of Minecraft, with real shipwrecks, treasure maps, dolphins, tridents and one very loud thunderstorm.

Read the opening pages

The greatest treasure hunts in history begin with a map. Ours began with a dolphin who wanted Dad’s lunch.

“Last one,” said Renata, holding a raw cod out over the side of the Plucky. “Make it count.”

The dolphin rolled up beside her boat, gray and shining, and took the fish right out of her hand. Then it flicked its tail and shot away south through the turquoise water.

“Follow that noble sea-hound!” I cried.

“It’s a dolphin,” said Renata, already paddling. “It wanted the cod.”

“It is a noble sea-hound who wanted the cod.”

“Feed a dolphin a fish,” Renata said, “and it swims to the nearest wreck. That’s not noble. That’s just how dolphins are.”

“Dolphins like showing off,” said Ravi from the back. “I respect that.”

So we followed it, all three boats. My fleet.

“Three boats is a fleet,” I said. “I checked.”

“Two boats and a lunch box,” said Renata.

I am Captain Dylan Lockett, I am eleven, and I wear a navy coat with brass buttons that I painted myself. My boat is the Swagger.

“The Dauntless,” I corrected myself out loud.

“Nobody calls it that,” said Renata.

“I call it that.”

“Nobody else,” said Renata, checking the sky for the second time in a minute. That’s Renata Solano, my first mate, with her black braid tucked under a sea-foam headscarf and a compass and a clock on her belt. She is the best boat-driver in Firstlight Harbor, and she knows it, and so does everyone else.

In the front of her boat sat my little sister, Effie, who is eight and wears my old yellow coat, two sizes too big. The crew’s spyglass hung on a cord around her neck. On her left shoulder rode Admiral, our red parrot.

“Admiral outranks me,” I told the dolphin, in case it was wondering. “Effie gave him the rank. I was not consulted.”

Last came Ravi Pillai in the Hamper, our chest boat, with round glasses, sawdust in his hair, and a crafting table strapped to his back like a backpack. The Hamper‘s chest was so full that the lid wouldn’t shut.

“Your dad packed enough cod for forty days,” said Ravi, pushing the lid down with his elbow. “We’re going out for one afternoon.”

“He always does that,” I said.

This morning Dad had stood on the wharf by the Galley, waving a fish. “Did you pack the cod? Pack the extra cod!”

That extra cod was now leading us to treasure, so I was not going to complain.

“Remind me why we’re in such a hurry,” said Ravi.

“Harbor Day,” I said grandly. “The day after tomorrow, the crew with the best find rings the harbor bell and leads the boat parade. Cordelia Prowse has led it two years running.”
