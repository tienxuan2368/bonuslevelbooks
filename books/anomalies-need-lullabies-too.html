Read the opening pages

At 1:33 AM, a tired little owl rings the bell. Every clock in the hospital stops.

Every night at seven, Tavo Castillo, his big sister Dulce and their best friend Abe clock in to Pawprint Hospital, their private server in a spooky night-shift animal-hospital game on Roblox. Dulce works the Night Window and Abe runs the cameras. Tavo is the Runner: he names every patient in three seconds flat and shuts every door.

Because now something lives in the vents. It rattles down the hall toward any open door, and when it gets there, the lamps go out.

Every hour, the owl comes back closer, and while it sits at the glass, the clocks won't move. And at 3:33, the night stops ending.

At home, Tavo is looking after the class hamster for the long weekend and learning the hard way that hamsters sleep all day. He is the loudest kid in third grade. To save the night shift, he may have to be the quietest.

Read the opening pages

Tavo Castillo carried the cage up the porch steps the way you carry a birthday cake: slowly, with both hands. His tongue poked through the gap where his front tooth used to be.

“Steady, mijo,” said Mom behind him.

“I AM steady,” said Tavo.

Inside the cage, under a mountain of paper bedding, something small and golden did not move at all.

His name was Captain Cheeks, and he was the third-grade class hamster. Every long weekend, one kid got to take him home, and this time, for the very first time, it was Tavo. There was no school on Monday, so that meant four whole nights.

Ms. Hollins had taped a care card to the cage. Tavo had read it seven times on the way home, and he read it again on the porch, out loud.

“’Hi! I’m Captain Cheeks. I am a night animal, so please let me sleep all day. Quiet voices near my cage, please. Fresh water every morning. A little food every evening. No picking me up on sleepovers. Just say hi!’”

He looked up. “How will he know I like him, if I can’t pick him up?”

“He’ll know,” said Mom. “Give him time.”

The screen door squeaked. Tavo stepped inside with the cage held high, and he did what he always did when he came home. He yelled.

“I’M HOME! CAPTAIN CHEEKS IS HOME! EVERYBODY—”

“Shh!” Mom pointed down the hall.

On Papá’s bedroom door hung a drawing of an owl in a nightcap, snoring. Under it, in Papá’s neat block capitals, it said DO NOT DISTURB. SLEEPING.

Papá was a vet nurse at the all-night animal hospital across town. He worked while everyone else slept, so he slept while everyone else was awake. This weekend, he was working four nights in a row.

“Sorry,” Tavo whispered. His whisper came out about as quiet as a leaf blower.

+ + +

They set the cage on Tavo’s dresser, away from the sunny window and the drafty door. Lentil padded in to see. She was twelve, with a gray muzzle, one ear up and one ear down, and a red bandana that never stayed straight. She sniffed the cage very politely.

“Out you go, old girl,” said Mom. “Dogs and hamsters are better friends with a door between them.”

Lentil went, and Mom shut the door.

Tavo pressed his face to the bars. “HI, CAPTAIN! I’M TAVO! I’M YOUR BEST FRIEND NOW!”

The bedding twitched. A tiny pink nose poked out, sniffed once and went straight back in.

“He doesn’t like me,” said Tavo.

“He’s asleep,” said Mom. “It’s four in the afternoon. That’s the middle of his night.”

Tavo sat down on the floor. At school, Ms. Hollins had a chart called the Voice-O-Meter, which went from 0 for silent all the way up to 5 for the playground. Tavo’s clip lived on 5. Some days it lived on 6, which wasn’t even on the chart.
