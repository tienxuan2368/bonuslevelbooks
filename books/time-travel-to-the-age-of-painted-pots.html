Read the opening pages

A heart pressed into ancient clay. A gate that opens only at sunset. One night to get home.

Anjali Rao hates guessing, and hates being wrong even more. For twenty-four days she has refused to set the Heartbreak sherd in the Gate of Eight Clocks, because she can't be sure which age it will open. When she finally steps through with Pilar, she finds Dialwood's own buried ruin alive: a ten-floor tower of painted clay, a candle in every window, and a town of potters on the night they break pots on purpose.

Then a pillager patrol comes down the road. A stubborn apprentice walks within ten strides of them in the dark, and Anjali must lead everyone home past five crossbows with a rule she isn't sure of and two arrows left. Back home, Elias guards the Gate alone while an enderman picks at the hill and the ceiling falls, block by block.

Read the opening pages

Guess: The Heartbreak sherd was made for a sad goodbye.

Because: The heart is cracked straight down the middle. Nobody draws that on a happy day.

Anjali Rao had drawn the Heartbreak sherd twenty-three times, and it still would not tell her a single thing.

It lay on the long table at the back of the Hall of Finds, in a nest of white wool, exactly where it had lain for twenty-four days. It was flat and red-brown and about the size of her palm, and pressed into its face was a heart with a crack running straight down the middle.

She pushed her round glasses up with one knuckle and leaned closer. At the edge of the heart there was a tiny oval dent, smooth as the inside of a spoon. Somebody had pressed this picture into wet clay with their own thumb, a long, long time ago, and she could see exactly where the thumb had been.

“Twenty-four,” she said, and started drawing again.

The afternoon sun came in long and gold through the windows, lighting up the shelves of pots, the barrels of finds and the long Timeline Wall, where only two labels hung so far. One was way over at the far left end: THE AGE OF FIRST FIRES · VISITED. The other was at the far right: THE LANTERN AGE. In between there was nothing but empty wall, and Anjali tried not to look at it too often.

Under the Timeline Wall hung a small spruce board that said WRONG GUESSES, with two cards pinned to it. Both of them were hers.

The front door banged open.

“Twenty-four days!” said Elias Hourigan.

He came in with his sandy hair sticking up at the crown, his spare green scarf flying and a brush stuck through his belt loop. He marched straight to the table and pointed at the sherd as if it had done something rude.

“It’s been sitting in that wool for twenty-four days,” he said, “and we haven’t even tried it once.”

“Twenty-four days and six hours,” said Pilar Ibarra from the window seat, holding up the crew’s clock on its cord. She was ten and small, with two curly puffs of hair and a brown apron that had far too many pockets. “And a bit.”

Under the table, Cog opened one eye. He was a red fox, orange as a pumpkin, with a white chest and chin, and he had been asleep in the table’s shade since lunch with a pencil in his mouth. It was Mr. Tallow’s pencil, and Mr. Tallow was still looking for it.

“I’m not putting it in the Gate,” said Anjali, “until I know where it goes.”

“We know where it goes,” said Elias. “The Age of Painted Pots, obviously, because it came out of the trench right next to the tower. Where else would it go?”
