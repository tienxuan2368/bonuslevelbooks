One loop. That's all it took to make the fastest Chiller on the server type "huh."

A brand-new map has dropped: the Mail Maze, a giant sorting center full of rolling belts, towering shelves and twelve long, straight aisles. Waiting there is ZipCode, the most famous Chiller in the game. It picks Skate every time, and in a straight line, nothing can outrun it.

Then Wilder, the Thaw Crew's loudest decoy, hops a belt and loops ZipCode three times in a row. Now he wants a whole round of it. But while Wilder chases the duel, his friends keep ending up frozen in frost pods, and the crew loses round after round.

To win, Wilder will have to learn the hardest move in the game: waiting. Luckily, he knows an old basset hound named Waffles who stops at every corner for a count of three.

Read the opening pages

It was seven o’clock on a Monday, and the sky outside the Furnace Room window was still light.

That was the first strange thing. The clocks had jumped forward an hour on Sunday, and now the evening didn’t know when to end. A stripe of pink sky showed through the little window above the old boiler.

The second strange thing was Wilder Haskins, who came down the basement stairs three at a time and smelled like six dogs.

“Don’t say it,” he said, stepping into the Furnace Room, which he insisted on calling Sector Zero.

“You smell like six dogs,” said Grace Adeyinka. She was drumming her fingers on the long folding table, the way she drummed them on every table she ever sat at.

“Five,” said Wilder. “Bean is a chihuahua, so Bean barely counts.”

Wilder’s mom walked dogs for half the neighborhood. Her helper was away all week, so every day after school, Wilder held three leashes and his mom held the other three.

“And Waffles stops at every corner,” Wilder said, dropping onto a saggy couch. His red hair stuck straight up in front, and his knee was already bouncing. “He’s the old basset hound. He doesn’t sniff anything. He just stops, like somebody unplugged him, and if you pull him, he sits down.”

“Then don’t pull him,” said Tolu.

Tolu was Grace’s little sister. She was eight, and she sat at the end of the couch with her purple backpack on, the way she always did. A keycard that said SCOUT hung from the zipper.

“I’m not built for waiting,” Wilder said. “I’m built for speed.”

Anika Desai was checking her big plastic wristwatch, and then the stripe of pink sky, and then her watch again.

“It’s seven-oh-two,” she said. “It looks like five.” She turned her laptop around so they could see. “And there’s a new map.”

The update had dropped that afternoon. On Anika’s screen, the vote pad showed a huge gray building full of belts, with a little red airplane on the roof.

“The Mail Maze,” Anika read. “A giant sorting center with twelve long aisles and belts everywhere.”

“Twelve long aisles,” Grace said slowly. “Long and straight?”

“Long and straight,” said Anika.

Everybody knew what that meant. They had learned it on Christmas Eve, in a hall full of icicles. One of the Chiller’s tricks was Skate: ice boots, and a burst of speed like gliding, for a count of three. Skate couldn’t jump, but in a long, straight hall it didn’t need to.

“Skate eats straight lines,” Tolu said quietly.

“Skate eats straight lines,” Grace agreed.

The boiler hummed in the corner. Then the stairs creaked and the toolbelt jingled, and Mr. Pappas came down with a roll of tape in one hand and his reading glasses pushed up on his head.
