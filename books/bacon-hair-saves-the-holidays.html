Read the opening pages

Everyone in Spawnbury thinks Nolan Tibbs just got here. He has lived there his whole life.

Nolan is the only kid in town who still wears the Starter Look: plain shirt, plain pants and brown hair in three flat strips that folks call bacon hair. His family runs the Welcome Hut beside the Spawn Pad. In four days it's Christmas Eve, the night more fresh spawns arrive than any other.

Then the Spawn Pad cracks and goes dark, right under Nolan's shoes.

Now newcomers land at random on any pad that's switched on, and a lazy visitor named Loosebolt keeps switching on the worst ones: an ice rink at the edge of the world and a frozen, slanted roof. The shiny kids say a bacon hair can't fix anything. To save Christmas Eve, Nolan needs plain grey bricks, a lot of nerve, his very loud little sister, his best friend and one switch nobody can reach.

Read the opening pages

Here is how you put a star on top of a Christmas tree in Spawnbury. You climb the ladder, you hold the star, and you anchor it.

If you skip the last part, the star falls, and things fall fast around here. I once saw a cup of cocoa hit the ground before the kid holding it finished saying “oops.”

So I anchored it. I pressed my thumb on the star’s middle brick until I felt the tiny click. An anchored brick never moves, not for bumps or crowds or even my little sister.

“Anchor it or lose it,” I said to nobody.

Builders say that, and I’m a builder. Mostly I’m the kid who hands out maps, but I build on the side.

From the top of the ladder, I could see all of Welcome Square in its December look. There were snow bricks on every roof, white and soft-looking. Paper stars hung all over the Welcome Tree, one for every fresh spawn we had welcomed this year.

The Welcome Tree is made of plain bricks, and Mrs. Ruiz painted it green for free. The star on top is mine. It has five points, and it’s made of plain grey bricks, anchored.

Below me, a girl with wings that blinked red and green stopped walking.

“Did you just get here?” she called up.

“I was born here,” I said.

“Aww,” she said. “Today?”

(That makes 2,341.)

She walked off before I could explain. People mostly do.

It’s my hair. It’s brown, in three flat strips that stick out like bacon in a pan. It’s the Starter Look, the look every fresh spawn arrives in, with a plain cream shirt, plain blue pants and brown shoes.

Most folks change their look by lunch on their first day. I never did, because I like it. It’s free, it never wears out, and it matches everybody’s first day.

“Nolan! Four days!” Mom called from the Welcome Hut.

She stood in the doorway in her bright orange vest, with a clipboard of maps in one arm. On the doorstep lay Pancake, our dog, lying so flat that he looked like a doormat that had learned to wag.

“I know, Mom!”

“Map, smile, wave!” she said. “Four days, people!”

Four days from now was Christmas Eve. On Christmas Eve, from the second the sun goes down, more fresh spawns arrive in Spawnbury than on any other night. Folks call it the Christmas Wave.

“Nobody knows why,” Pop always says. “I think everybody’s wish comes true on the same night.”

Last year, Mom’s tally board went up by one thousand, nine hundred and four in one night. We ran out of cocoa twice.

“IS THE STAR ON?” yelled my sister, Tinsley. She’s six, and she only has one volume. “IS IT SHINY? CAN I TOUCH IT? WHY IS IT GREY?”
