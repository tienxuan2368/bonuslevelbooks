Bluet and the Firefly Night

A Read-Aloud Fairy Chapter Book for Kids 6-8 About Kindness

by Posy Larkmoor

Ages 6–8Level 1Gentle

Read the opening pages

Bluet has waited seven whole summers for Firefly Night. Now it's going to rain.

Under the roots of an old azalea tree, in the soft green moss of the blocky world, lives a village of tiny fairies. Bluet is the smallest of them all. Tonight, she is finally old enough to fly to Plip Pond and watch the fireflies rise.

But gray clouds are piling up over the hill. Wet wings are heavy, and in the rain, every fairy must fly home. And everyone knows you can't catch a firefly. So how do you bring Firefly Night home?

With careful Beet and his leaf map, speedy Zaley, sleepy Old Oxeye, a little blue allay named Chime who brings back everything, and a big, mossy sniffer named Duffle, Bluet sets out to save the night the fairy way: by looking closer at how the world really works.

Read the opening pages

FIREFLY NIGHT!

POND AT SUNSET.

NO SNORING.

— by order of Old Oxeye

Bluet read the sign four times. Then she hung upside down from a root and read it once more, just to be sure.

It still said the same thing.

“Firefly Night is tonight!” she sang. “Tonight, tonight, TONIGHT!”

The sign hung on the Old Azalea. The Old Azalea was a tree at the edge of a flower forest. Tulips and daisies grew all around it. Its long roots curled deep into soft green moss. Little round doors sat between the roots. Glow berries hung over the doors like tiny lamps.

This was Dewlight Hollow. It was the home of the fairies.

Big folk walked by it all the time. They never saw a thing. When a fairy holds still in the flowers, she looks just like a petal.

Bluet was the smallest fairy in the Hollow. She was as tall as one azure bluet flower, and not one petal taller. She had sky-blue hair in two round puffs. She wore a white petal dress with a pale-blue hem and a yellow sash. A little string pouch bounced on her hip. Her four white wings were always on the go.

Today, they would not hold still at all.

Bluet knew all about Firefly Night.

“Once a year,” she told a moss sprout, “the whole Hollow flies down to Plip Pond after sunset. We sit in the dark, very still. And we watch the fireflies rise!”

The moss sprout did not say a thing. Moss is a very good listener.

“But you have to be seven summers old to go,” said Bluet. “And guess what? I am seven summers old!”

She had been seven summers old for nine whole days.

“Nine days,” said Beet. “I counted.”

Beet was Bluet’s best friend. He was tall for a fairy, with dark curls under a round red cap. His moss-green coat had six pockets. He knew what was in every one. A rolled-up leaf map lived under his arm.

“Nine days is so long,” said Bluet.

“Nine is a lot of days,” said Beet. “Nine is almost ten.”

“I waited seven summers,” said Bluet. “And nine days. Why is tonight so slow? Why?”

“I don’t know,” said Beet. “Let me check.” He looked at his map. “Hmm. My map does not say.”

A bee buzzed by. It was as big as Bluet.

“Hello, little somebody!” Bluet called. “It’s Firefly Night!”

The bee did not stop. Bees are busy. But it hummed a bit louder as it went. Bluet was sure that counted.

Then something pink zipped over their heads.

“Zoom first, think second!” yelled Zaley.

Flump. She landed face first in the moss.
