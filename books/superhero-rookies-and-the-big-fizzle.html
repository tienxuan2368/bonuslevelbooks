Read the opening pages

The Fizzles are ranked dead last. Tonight, they're the only heroes left.

Rafi Okafor is ten, and his brand-new superpower is… wind. At Blockburst Academy, every student's power works exactly like something real in the blocky world: a wind charge, a Swiftness potion, Invisibility, the Turtle Master's tough shell. On his very first day, Rafi's gust wrecks the Showcase, and his squad lands at the bottom of the Hero Board with a nickname nobody wants: the Fizzles.

Then, at the Welcome Feast, somebody swaps the honey for milk. One gulp, and every hero's power switches off until sunrise. Every hero except four rookies who were late because they were cleaning up Rafi's mess.

That same night, two little kids from town sneak into the Trials beneath the academy, where the breezes live. Speedy Frankie, dramatic Gigi, slow-and-steady Wendell and Rafi have four puffs of wind, twenty tough seconds, one very hungry runner and a glowing allay who loves cookies. To save the day, they'll need every "useless" power they've got, the rules of the world, and each other.

Read the opening pages

IN THE HALLS.

The sign hung over the doors of the Great Hall. Rafi tucked his hands under his arms, just to be safe.

The Great Hall was tall and bright, with glass walls and a polished stone floor. Twenty-four first-years stood around in nervous clumps, comparing Bursts.

“Mine’s Glowmark,” said a boy. “I can make stuff glow.”

“Mine’s Nightsight,” said a girl. “Tell me when it gets dark.”

Rafi didn’t tell anyone his, because wind was not something you bragged about.

On the far wall hung the Hero Board, which was six empty signs in a row, numbered one to six. By tonight, every first-year would be on one of those signs in a squad of four. The best squad would go at the top, and the worst at the bottom.

+ * +

“Enter Gigi, stage left!”

A girl swept into the hall with both arms in the air. She had pale skin, a great many freckles and a long red braid. She wore a purple tunic with a curly amethyst trim and a tall magenta cap. She was holding a cookie.

She bowed. No one clapped.

“That’s all right,” she said. “You’re shy. I’m Gigi Larsen, my Burst is Fade, and it is spectacular, which means amazing to look at. Watch.”

She faded away, but her cap did not. It hung in the air at head height all by itself, with near-white swirls drifting around it. The cookie hung right beside it.

“Your hat’s still there,” said Frankie, who had appeared out of nowhere with a mouthful of kelp.

“I’m aware,” said the hat. “It’s my signature look.”

“And your cookie.”

The cookie took a bite of itself.

Behind them, someone very large cleared his throat. A tall, broad boy was waiting patiently to get past. He had round glasses, a moss-green tunic with a copper trim and a turtle shell on his head like a helmet.

“Sorry,” he said. “I didn’t want to step on the hat. I’m Wendell Tran, and my Burst is Shell. It came a year late, so I’m eleven. You can’t rush a pumpkin.”

“I’m Rafi,” said Rafi. “Gust. It’s… wind.”

“Wind is good,” Wendell said. “Wind carries seeds.”

Rafi had never once thought about that.

+ * +

whoosh

A boy shot in through a high open window. He was gliding with his arms out, and a shimmer like beetle wings flashed on his back. He had slick blond hair and a white tunic with a gold trim. He soared over every head in the hall.

It was the most heroic thing Rafi had ever seen.

Then the boy flew straight into the wall.

Beckett Rowe had burst into the hall. Technically, he had burst into the wall.
