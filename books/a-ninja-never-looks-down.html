Read the opening pages

A ninja never looks down. Bash wrote that rule himself. It never helped.

Bamboo Bend whispers about the ninja nobody ever sees. The truth is three kids in indigo and Thud, a baby panda with a very busy nose. Their secret teacher, who signs every note "Nobody," has built them a line of planks through the treetops of the cherry ridge. Nomi crosses it in a blink. Bash, who is afraid of heights, freezes halfway.

The next night, the rival Roof Kings climb the tallest roof in the valley to hang their sign. A creeper blows up their ladder right where Bash is standing. Now three kids are stuck in the dark, spiders are climbing, more creepers are coming, and the Roof Kings think the ninja did it on purpose.

To bring them down before dawn, the clan will have to tear apart Nobody's own course, trust a white cat named Smudge, and send the one ninja who is scared of heights to the top.

Read the opening pages

The pot had not been there yesterday.

It stood at the foot of the Petal Steps, where Nobody’s training run always began. It was a round clay pot with little pictures pressed into its sides, and it sat in the fallen petals as if it had grown there.

“Six hundred and two steps,” whispered Bash. “From the Rafters to the pot.”

They had crossed three roofs and two trapdoors and climbed the slope as fast as they could go without running, but nobody had been waiting, and the lights in the trees were all dark now.

Nomi reached into the pot and found paper, folded small and tight.

The clan had a teacher they had never met. The teacher left missions on paper, always three short lines in neat, square handwriting, always signed the same way. They called the teacher Nobody, because that was the name on every scroll.

Lu held up the lantern, and they read it together.

Above the petals

one plank wide from tree to tree.

Cross before the bell.

— Nobody

Then they looked up.

Nobody’s Course had always started at this spot, with ladders and hay and trapdoors leading down over the roofs to the Rafters. But tonight something new went up.

A tower of scaffolding rose out of the petals, pale yellow bamboo frames stacked one on another, straight up into the crown of the first cherry tree. From the top, a line of cherry planks ran away through the pink leaves. It was one block wide, and it went from tree to tree to tree, high above the grass, up the ridge and out of sight.

Every few trees, a target block was set into a trunk, with red and white rings painted on it. Beside each target sat a lamp, joined to it by a thin line of red dust.

“That’s what we saw,” said Lu. “The lights.”

“A high line,” said Nomi. “Nobody built us a high line.”

Bash said nothing, but in the lantern light his face had gone the color of old snow.

“It’s easy,” said Nomi. “It’s just a roof, only thinner.”

“Roofs have sides,” said Bash. “Roofs have a whole house under them.”

Lu took a pink petal from the pouch at her belt and dropped it into the pot. One petal meant yes, we’ll do it.

Bash walked around the bottom of the tower and touched the lowest frame very gently, the way you would touch a sleeping wolf.

“This bottom piece holds up all the others,” he said. “Break it, and the whole tower comes down at once. Every piece. So nobody kick it.”

“Why would anybody kick it?” said Lu.

“Somebody always does.”

Thud had already found a soft patch of petals at the foot of the tower, since pandas could not climb scaffolding. Bash took two stalks of bamboo from his pack and laid them in front of him.
