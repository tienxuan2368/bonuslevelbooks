Read the opening pages

A ninja never sneezes. Nobody told the panda.

In Bamboo Bend, good deeds happen at night, and the whole village argues about the mysterious ninja nobody ever sees. The truth is three kids in indigo and Thud, a baby panda who sneezes about every twenty-five seconds.

Then a three-line poem from their secret teacher, who signs every note "Nobody," sends them to Flint Ridge. Something bright blue is locked in a cage at the pillager outpost, and it has to be home before the dawn bell.

Nomi wants a perfect, silent mission, so she leaves Thud behind. It goes wrong fast. A single snowball turns every crossbow in the camp toward Lu. The Roof Kings spot a small ninja who hums. And the patrol follows them to the Old Bridge.

To bring the allay home, the clan will have to break one of its own rules on purpose and find out what a good sneeze is really for.

Read the opening pages

The newest paper on the notice board was written in big, careful letters.

THE NINJA IS TWO NINJAS. ONE OF THEM IS ENORMOUS. — Mrs. Briggs, the baker

Half of Bamboo Bend stood in the Bell Square to read it, and the other half stood behind the well, staring at a torch.

“That corner has been dark since my grandmother was a girl,” said the cooper.

“Dark corners are trouble,” said Mrs. Briggs, dusting the crumbs off her hands. “Twice this spring a zombie came shuffling out of that one. Twice!”

“And twice I ran across this square in my slippers to ring the bell,” said Mr. Owusu. “In my slippers, on a cold night, with the wrong key.”

Now the corner had a torch, and in a lit corner, nothing climbs out of the dark.

“So who put it there?” asked the cooper.

“The ninja,” said everyone at once.

Nomi Hara stood at the back of the crowd with her hands behind her. At midnight those hands had pressed that torch to that wall. At dawn they had still smelled of smoke, and she had washed them twice.

Nobody saw.

A ninja never waits to be thanked. It was one of the oldest rules on the clan’s list, and Nomi liked it. She also liked the crowd, more than she wanted to admit. So she kept her face very still.

Next to her, Bash Owusu was trying to look small. Bash was eleven and built like a bookshelf, so it was not going well. He bent his knees and rounded his back until he looked like a bookshelf that felt bad about something.

“Enormous,” whispered Lu Marchetti, on Nomi’s other side. “She means you.”

“She means a ninja,” Bash whispered back. “Ninjas are not me.”

“You’re the biggest ninja there is.”

“Shh,” said Nomi, without moving her lips.

Lu touched the corner of the paper. When she took her hand away, a small orange fingerprint stayed behind. Lu’s parents ran the dye works by the river, and every day her fingers came home a new color. Today they were orange.

Something sneezed under the Quiet Ladle.

Hff. Hff. Hfff-SHOO.

The stew stall’s tablecloth jumped, and a baby panda rolled out from under it. He sat up and blinked his teary eyes at the crowd. His nose was running, because his nose was always running.

“Bless you, Thud,” said Grandma Hara from behind her pots. “Bless you, and bless your nose, it never gets a day off, does it, poor thing.”

Everyone in Bamboo Bend knew Thud, because he was Bash’s panda. He followed bamboo, he followed Bash, and he sneezed about twice a minute. What nobody knew was where Thud went at night.
