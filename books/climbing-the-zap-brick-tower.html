Read the opening pages

Fall off, float down, try again. That's how the Tower works.

In the toy-bright town of Toehold stands the Tower: the tallest, brightest, wobbliest tower anywhere. Every few minutes it shuffles into a brand-new climb. There are no halfway spots. One toe on a red zap brick, and you float all the way back down to the pink Soft Spot.

Today is Climb Day. Everyone who reaches the Top before the last BONG gets their name on the Top Wall, and Rio Asante wants his name up there more than anything. There's just one problem. A blinking zap-brick floor called Sir Blinks-a-Lot keeps showing up again and again, and it keeps sending Rio back to the start.

Lila has a plan (it's wrong). Paco falls the most and yells "AGAIN!" Coach Bunny cheers every fall. And when the Big Clock starts racing on the very last climb, Rio has a choice to make: zoom to the Top alone, or stop for a friend who is stuck.

Read the opening pages

Rio Asante is in the air, and he is not waiting for anything.

He leaps off the purple floor. He lands on Floor 5 with both feet. Floor 5 is as far as Rio has ever gotten.

Rio flaps his arms like a happy chicken. “I’ve GOT this!”

Rio has short black twists and a bright yellow sweatband. He wears an orange zip-up jacket and green high-tops. His laces are double-knotted. That’s Coach’s rule.

Floor 5 is lime green. It is a long, skinny bridge with three gates standing across it, one after another. Each gate is made of red zap bricks, and the bricks glow like cherry candy.

The gates hum. Then they go dark. Then they glow again.

“Rio, WAIT!” yells Lila from Floor 4. “It’s blinking!”

Waiting is not something Rio does. Not ever.

He runs at the first gate while the bricks are still glowing, and the tip of his sneaker taps a red brick.

Fzzt.

Oh, Rio.

A big buzzy jolt zips up his leg. It doesn’t hurt one bit, but it makes his teeth feel fizzy, like soda. Then the Tower lets go of him, and Rio floats.

Down and down he goes, slow as a feather in no hurry.

He floats past Floor 4, the purple floor, where Lila covers her eyes. He floats past Floor 3 and the green steps. His whole climb is gone, every single floor of it. That’s the rule. There are no halfway spots on the Tower.

Floomp. Back to the Soft Spot!

The Soft Spot is a huge pink landing pad at the bottom of the Tower, as squishy as a giant marshmallow. Every climb starts here, and every fall ends here.

A pug waddles over to Rio. He is fawn with a black face, a curly tail and a teal bandana. His name is Dumpling, and he licks Rio right on the nose.

Dumpling’s face says: Welcome back. Again.

“I was SO close,” Rio tells the sky.

Something small floats down next to him and lands with a squish. It is Paco Mercado, Lila’s little brother, who is five and a half. He has a red-and-white striped shirt and a puffy knit hat with a huge pom-pom. The pom-pom bounces when he lands, and it bounces a lot.

“AGAIN!” yells Paco, and he runs straight back to the bottom step.

Paco holds the Toehold falling record for kids his age, and he is very proud of it.

“FWEET!”

That is Coach Bunny. She is tall and round, with silver hair in a high bun and a teal tracksuit with white stripes. On one hand she wears a giant foam high-five hand. Around her neck hangs a whistle with no pea inside, so Coach just yells the whistle part herself.
