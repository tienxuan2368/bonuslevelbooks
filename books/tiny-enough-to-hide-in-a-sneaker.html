The best seeker on Quince Street has to hide. In the dark. From the one boy who knows every hiding spot.

Sol Benítez can find anybody in under a minute. Hiding is another story: she can't stand still, and every time her best friend Kenta Ward has been It, he has found her. Tonight, in the Roblox game Hide and Seek Extreme, the game finally flips the Showdown. Kenta is It, in his Night Moth suit, with two red-eyed cameras he can look through from anywhere in the room.

The room is the Sleepover Bedroom, and it is lights out. One flashlight. A dark under the bed that goes back forever. A secret spot in the wall that a sixth-grader swears nobody can find, and that Sol promised she would never use. Every time a red eye lights up, Sol has to freeze. Every time it goes dark, she wants to run. Kenta is counting on that.

Four minutes. Two red eyes. One hider left.

SLEEPOVER FRIDAY · GAME NIGHT AT 7:00

I could find anybody on Quince Street in under a minute. Hiding was a different story.

“Sol is the WORST hider on Quince Street,” Lupe told the whole bus stop.

The bus stop was a pole with a sign on it, at the corner where Quince Street met the big road. There were six kids under it that Friday morning, plus one backpack shaped like a turtle, which belonged to Lupe. My little sister was six. She wore her unicorn headband on top of her winter hat, because she never took it off. She said everything at full volume.

“Lupe,” I said. “Inside voice.”

“We’re OUTSIDE,” said Lupe. Then she held up nine fingers, very carefully. “Kenta found her NINE times.”

She didn’t have to say the number. I counted everything, and I had definitely counted that one.

This was how it worked. Every Friday was Game Night, and on Game Night we played our hide-and-seek game on the Friday Server. Every round, the game picked one player to be It. When it picked Kenta, and I was hiding, Kenta found me. He had done it nine times, and he had never needed more than a minute.

Kenta was standing next to me with his gray hood up and his sleeves pulled down over his hands. He lived downstairs from me at 12 Quince Street, in a house so narrow I could touch both hallway walls at once. He was the best hider in the world. I had been It against him in thirteen Showdowns, and I had never found him once.

“It was eight,” Kenta said quietly. “One time you sneezed.”

“That counts,” said Lupe.

“Ref says it counts,” said Vera Goodwin.

Vera lived across the street. Her box braids were tied back with a red band. Her silver whistle hung on its lanyard, even at the bus stop. Under one arm she carried a composition notebook. On the cover it said THE SHOWDOWN BOOK, in marker. Under her other arm was a rolled-up green sleeping bag, because tonight she was sleeping over at my house.

“Nine,” Vera said. “I have it written down.”

That was when Grady Fitch showed up.

Grady was a sixth-grader, the oldest kid at our stop, and he wanted everybody to remember it. He wasn’t mean, exactly, but he talked like everything he said was a fact.

“You guys still play on your little server?” he asked.

“The Friday Server,” said Vera. “Yes.”

“I play public,” said Grady. “I’ve played every hiding game on Roblox. My It suit is the Rumbler. Nobody has ever beaten the Rumbler. Not one time.”

I should have kept my mouth shut. I didn’t. “Kenta’s never been found in a Showdown,” I said. “Thirteen in a row.”
