Read the opening pages

In Kart Canyon, every kart has the same top speed. Nobody told the kids who won't pick Nolan Tibbs for a team.

Nolan has lived in Spawnbury his whole life, but he still wears the plain Starter Look every newcomer arrives in: brown hair in three flat strips that folks call bacon hair. So when Cup Week starts, nobody wants him in their kart. Except his best friend, Dalia, who has never driven one either.

Their first kart falls apart on the first ramp. A lazy visitor named Loosebolt bends the canyon's rules to watch things fall: welds come loose, warnings vanish, and karts get stuck in the lava. Then Dalia comes up short on the Big Leap, lands on a crumbling ledge above the void and quits.

On the last day, the one-of-a-kind Kart Cup rolls toward Split Gap in a kart with nobody driving it, hugged by a new kid who doesn't know how to get out. The only driver close enough is Dalia.

Read the opening pages

“IS IT FAR? IS IT FAST? CAN I DRIVE? CAN MAREN DRIVE? CAN PANCAKE DRIVE?”

“No, no, no, no, and absolutely not,” I said.

We were walking up Portal Row, at the north end of town. Portal Row is a line of tall arches, and each one carries you to a different world. Mom had said Tinsley, Maren and Pancake could come and watch, as long as everybody came back through the arch before dark.

“Before dark,” Mom had said twice. “Map, smile, wave, and before dark.”

The arch to Kart Canyon is red rock with a checkered flag painted on top. Dalia grabbed my hand and pulled me through.

Fwip.

Kart Canyon is red. The rock is red, the dust is red, and the sky turns red early, like it’s in a hurry for sundown. A road wound along the top of the canyon like a ribbon somebody dropped. On one side of the road was the canyon wall. On the other side was the Edge, and past the Edge there was nothing at all. Every world has an edge somewhere. Kart Canyon just doesn’t hide it.

Far across the canyon stood a big flat-topped rock, and between us ran a crack that went down to nothing.

“That’s Split Gap,” Dalia said. “You jump it.”

“In what?”

“In a kart, Nolan. Keep up.”

Near the arch was a long wooden shed with letters painted along the top. Whoever painted them ran out of room at the end, so the last word was squashed.
