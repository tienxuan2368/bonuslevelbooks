Read the opening pages

A shiny new Welcome Center just opened across the square, and it welcomes every fresh spawn in ten seconds flat.

Nolan Tibbs has worn the plain bacon-hair Starter Look his whole life. His mom runs the Welcome Hut, where every newcomer gets a map, a smile, a wave and their own name said out loud. But the Center has nine glass pads, and in Spawnbury, pads choose at random. Now nine out of ten newcomers never reach the Hut, and Mom starts to think her slow, plain welcome isn't worth much anymore.

Then the Center's loose arrows point somewhere new. Loosebolt, the rule-bending visitor who loves watching things fall, is turning them toward the Ember Run, a lava obstacle world where every runner gets one brick and the arches go dark at sundown. And Romy, a new girl who does exactly what every sign says, has followed them in.

Mom has mapped every world she has never visited. Now she has to walk into one.

Read the opening pages

My mom says “Welcome!” to everybody. I mean everybody. She says it to people who popped onto the Spawn Pad ten seconds ago. She says it to people who have lived next door for twenty years.

TOP 3 THINGS MOM HAS WELCOMED THIS YEAR

3. A lamp post, because it was standing near the pad.

2. Kingsley Glossop’s hat, which he left on a bench.

1. Me, every single morning, in my own kitchen.

That morning she said it to the last snow brick of the winter. It slid off the Welcome Hut roof and landed by the door with a soft thump. Mom glanced at it and said, “Welcome!”

“It’s leaving, Mom,” I said. “That’s the opposite of arriving.”

“Then welcome to spring,” she said, and stepped over it with her clipboard of maps.

I’m Nolan Tibbs. I’m eleven, and I’ve lived in Spawnbury my whole life. I still wear the Starter Look, the plain look everybody has on their first day. That means brown hair in three flat, wavy strips that stick out like bacon in a pan. It also means a cream T-shirt and plain blue pants. Folks call it bacon hair, and most of them think I got here this morning.

Our family runs the Welcome Hut, the little booth-house right beside the Spawn Pad. Inside, it smells like cocoa. There’s a tally board with the name of every fresh spawn we’ve ever welcomed, chalked small. There’s a lamp in the window that never goes out, and there’s Mom’s map wall.

The map wall covers the whole back of the Hut. It has a map of every world you can reach through Portal Row. That’s the line of tall arches at the north end of town. Snowcap and Kart Canyon are up there, and worlds I’ve only heard about. Every map is covered in tiny notes in Mom’s handwriting.

She draws them from stories. Every racer and rider who comes back through Portal Row stops at the Hut for cocoa, and Mom asks, “How was it? Which way did the path go?” Then she draws it.

As far as I know, she’s never been to a single one.

Pop sat in his chair by the window, next to the lamp, in his own Starter Look and his grey cardigan. He’s older than the Shine Mile. He keeps a plain grey brick in his pocket for luck, and he was polishing it on his sleeve.

My little sister, Tinsley, was lying on the floor under the map wall, kicking her feet. She’s six and loud. She wears every sparkle she can find, with one plain grey stripe down the middle.

“Can I do the welcome?” she yelled. “Can I? Can I do the WHOLE THING?”
