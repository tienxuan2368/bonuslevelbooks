Read the opening pages

Mina Nightshade told her little brother to be quiet. Just once. Then he turned into a bat.

For years, seven-year-old Barney has jumped off the library ladder shouting "BAT TIME!" and landed in a puff of chicken feathers. But two nights before the school play, he jumps without a sound, and this time it works. Now the loudest kid in Hollowbrook is a small brown bat who flies away from anyone who comes near. And he can't turn back.

Grandmother says a first bat must come home before the sun rises three times. Then Barney follows the wild bats down a crack in the cellar wall, deep into the Old Mineshaft, where the cobwebs grow thickest and something in the dark is making spiders four at a time.

Mina has her sword. Her best friend Kofi has a pickaxe and a bag of torches. The family cat has opinions. But everything the Nightshades own was made for zombies, and these spiders walk straight through the webs.

A funny, truly spooky family adventure set in the blocky world of Minecraft, with cave spiders, a silent creeper, a cloud of bats and one very rude wall of gravel. For grown-ups: short chapters, easy reading, real shivers with no gore, and a warm story about being proud of who you are.

Read the opening pages

My little brother turned into a bat just after midnight on Thursday.

That was the good news.

The bad news was everything that came after. And the worst news, the part I haven’t told anyone until now, is that it was my fault.

It started that afternoon, with a list on the school door.

Hollowbrook School has one room, eleven windows and one teacher, and the teacher, Mr. Quimby, had written a play.

HOLLOWBROOK’S FIRST NIGHT. A play in one act. Saturday, at sunset, on the school steps.

“At sunset!” the Mayor had shouted from his hay bale by the bell. “With the real moon rising behind it! Real scenery, folks, and free of charge!”

Everybody clapped, though my family didn’t need to, because sunset is the one time of day that is fair to everybody.

I found my name on the list. NARRATOR: Wilhelmina Nightshade.

“You have a very dry voice, Wilhelmina,” Mr. Quimby told me. “It’s perfect for history.”

The Narrator had sixty-one lines. I counted twice.

Tragic.

Then I looked for my brother.

VILLAGE BELL: Barney Nightshade.

The Village Bell had one line. The line was “DING-DONG!”

“It’s the most important line in the whole play,” Barney told everyone on the walk home. “Without the bell, nobody knows it’s bedtime. Without the bell, everybody stays out in the DARK.”

He told me, and he told two villagers at the well, who said “Hmm.” He told Clank, our village iron golem, who listened politely and creaked.

I should tell you who we are.

I’m Mina Nightshade. I’m eleven. I have a black bob with one white streak, red-violet eyes and a short black cape with a crimson lining. By day I wear a sun hat as wide as a cake, with an iron helmet hidden inside. My family and the sun do not get along.

My family is night folk. Vampires, if you want the short word. We drink berry juice, we see in the dark, and we never bite anybody, ever.

Barney is seven. He has a round face and a cowlick that points at the ceiling. His cape is so long he trips on it twice a day. He is the loudest person in Hollowbrook. Possibly in the world. Nobody has measured the world.

By midnight, he had said his line about four hundred times.

“DING-DONG!” in the hall. “DING-DONG!” on the stairs. “DING-DONG!” through the keyhole of the library, where I was trying to learn sixty-one lines.

Up on the roof, the phantoms were back, the way they come for my family every night. Phantoms hunt anyone who hasn’t slept for three nights, and my family hasn’t slept at night in about four hundred years. Their screeches scraped across the roof like chalk.
