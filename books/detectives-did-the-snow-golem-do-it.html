Read the opening pages

Every gift on the mountain has turned to snow, and the whole town says the snow golem did it.

On the morning before Gift Eve, the twelve gift chests at the Whitecap Market Hall should be full of presents. Instead, eleven hold a single snowball each, and a trail of snow runs straight to Sergeant Snowbank, the snow golem who guards the door. If the gifts aren't back by dusk tomorrow, the Hall on the snowy slopes will close to the town for good.

Jory Pennant doesn't buy it. Snow golems have sticks for arms. With Imani, who tests everything with redstone, Wynn, who knows what every mob will do next, and Wallop, a one-horned goat with strong opinions, the club keeps watch through a snowy night. Strays wait on the ridge. Powder snow swallows anyone without the right boots. And a hidden room shuts behind two detectives in the dark.

Every clue works the way it does in Minecraft, and before the reveal the book stops to ask: CAN YOU SOLVE IT?

Read the opening pages

This is the case file for the Gift Eve snow. I am writing it down because a whole mountain blamed someone with sticks for arms, and somebody should fix that in ink.

Case note: at sunrise on the day before Gift Eve, eleven gifts on the Whitecap Slopes turned into snow.

We had climbed the Lantern Path in the dark, the whole town together, because in Riverlatch winter only means rain. If you want snow, you have to climb for it.

Lanterns hang on spruce posts all the way up the path, and Dad patted every post as he passed.

“Lanterns,” he grumbled. “No switch. No detector. No keeper. They just sit there, burning. Showing off.”

“You love them,” said Mom.

“I respect them,” said Dad. “That’s different.”

Near the top, Wynn grabbed my sleeve and pointed at the ridge. A skeleton stood up there against the pink sky, pale blue and ragged, with a bow in its hands.

“Stray,” Wynn whispered. “A skeleton that got stuck in powder snow too long. It turns into that. Keep walking. Don’t stop.”

We kept walking, and the stray shot once. The arrow thunked into a post two blocks from Mrs. Lowe and her pies. Then the first sun touched the ridge, and the stray began to smoke, and then it was only smoke.

The Whitecap Market Hall sits on a flat shoulder of the mountain. It is long and gray, with a spruce roof under a foot of snow. The goat-keepers built it, and every winter they let Riverlatch hold its market inside.

We were ten blocks from the doors when somebody inside screamed.

It was Scoop. Her real name is Lorna Kettering, she is twelve, and she runs the Rumor Board back home. She had come up the path early and alone, with her notebook. Now she was standing at the back of the Hall, holding up the lid of a chest.

“SNOW!” she shouted. “Sources say, no, I say! It’s SNOW!”

The back wall of the Hall is the Gift Wall. Twelve chests sit in a spruce frame, with a sign over each one. On the first day of the market, twelve givers put one gift each inside. At dusk on Gift Eve comes the Giving, when names are drawn and everybody opens a chest that isn’t theirs.

The sign over Scoop’s chest said PENNANT, so that one was ours.

Mrs. Garrow came in through the side door with snow on her boots. She is the Keeper of the Hall: tall, with a gray braid and a brown leather coat. She walked along the Gift Wall and lifted every lid, one after another.

One snowball. One snowball. One snowball.
