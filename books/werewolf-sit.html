Read the opening pages

In the village of Mossrock, the most dangerous word is SIT. Especially on the night of the full moon.

Shep Howell is eleven, the second-fastest kid in town, and a werewolf. So are his mom and his little sister. Every full moon, the Howell pack slips into the deep spruce woods to chase skeletons away from the village, and nobody in Mossrock ever finds out.

Then Mr. Pettibone announces the village's first-ever Moonlight Games, under "the brightest light there is." The full moon.

Now Shep has to hide pointy ears under a giant hat, keep his tail from thumping the school bench, dodge two spitting llamas, and run the last leg of the Lantern Relay before the moon climbs over the trees. And when the moon is that full, one firm "SIT!" folds a werewolf's knees flat. In Mossrock, that's how every race begins.

Then Gil, the slowest kid on Shep's team, wanders off the course and into the dark woods, right where the armored skeletons are gathering. Shep has to choose: win the race, or go back for his friend.

Read the opening pages

I’m three steps behind Brynn Dalgaard, and three steps is a lot.

We’re racing down the spruce lane to school, past the pumpkin patch and over the low fence. My red scarf is flying, my boots are slapping, and the cold air tastes like pine sap and chimney smoke.

Brynn is twelve, and her legs are longer than my whole body. Okay, not really, but it feels that way when she slaps the library hall door first, again.

“No offense,” she says, fixing one of the two silver clips in her hair, “but you start too fast. You always run out of go.”

“I don’t run out of go,” I say. I say it bent over with my hands on my knees, running out of air, which is different.

I’m Shep Howell. I’m eleven, and I’m the second-fastest kid in Mossrock. That’s like coming in second at a cake-eating contest. I still get cake, but somebody else gets more.

Hana Mori strolls up behind us, because Hana doesn’t run anywhere. Her black braids swing with blue ribbons, and a brush for digging up treasure sticks out of her belt.

“You could try pacing yourself,” she says.

“Pacing is for people who like losing slowly.”

“Pacing is for people who like winning at the end.” She pats my shoulder. “Plan first. Panic second.”

Hana has been my best friend since we were six, and she knows everything about me, and I mean everything. We’ll get to that.

Then I hear a rattle coming up the lane. Clack-clack, clack-clack.

It isn’t a skeleton; it’s Gil Bixby.

Gil is nine, and his pockets are full of bones that he fishes out of the creek. Bones hardly ever come up on a hook, but Gil doesn’t care. He has nineteen of them, and when he runs, he sounds like a skeleton falling down the stairs.

“Shep!” he yells. “I found tracks! Big ones! Big Pale was down at the creek!”

“Wow, Big Pale at the creek,” I say.

I know Big Pale was at the creek getting a drink, because Big Pale was me.

Don’t tell Gil.

° • °

The library hall is our school, and today it smells like ink, damp wool and a sugar cookie. The cookie is in Gil’s hand, right next to a bone.

Mr. Pettibone is standing on his chair at the front of the room. He’s our teacher, and he’s also Mossrock’s games captain, which means he loves races more than anybody alive, except me.

He lifts his goat horn to his lips and blows. Bwaaa!

Every kid jumps. In the back corner, the Librarian drops a book and hums, “Hrmm.”

“MOSSROCK!” Mr. Pettibone booms. “I have NEWS!”
