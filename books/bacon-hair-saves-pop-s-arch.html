Read the opening pages

Everybody laughed when Pop said he built the first arch in any world. Nolan laughed too.

Nolan Tibbs has worn the plain bacon-hair Starter Look his whole life, and so has his grandpa, who tells every story a little taller than it happened. So when the arch to the Old World starts to lean, and Keeper Hale says it will go dark for good at sundown on the fifth day, nobody listens to Pop's plan.

The Old World is the loosest place there is: nothing there is anchored. And Loosebolt, the rule-bending visitor who loves watching things fall, has found it. A tower comes down. A sign slides into the empty sky. Then the arch itself falls, with everyone on the wrong side of it.

Now the only instructions are an old man's tall tale, and the only one who remembers every word arrived four days ago.

Read the opening pages

“Back on the First Day,” Pop said, “I built an arch on top of a hill.”

Two fresh spawns sat on the bench outside the Welcome Hut with cocoa in their hands. They were about three hours old. They couldn’t tell yet if Pop was about to say something important or fall asleep.

Pop is my grandpa. Nobody knows how old he is, but the Shine Mile is younger. He has kept the Starter Look since forever, with a plain grey cardigan on top and a lucky brick in one pocket. He tells stories slowly, and in the middle he whistles through his teeth, so you know a good part is coming.

“A hill of plain grey bricks,” he said. “Then I laid eleven stones over the hill. Five up each side, one on top. Every stone had a face, so I’d know where it went.”

“Stones don’t have faces,” said one of the fresh spawns.

“These did. Up the left side went the Flat One, the Fat One, the Loaf, the Smile and the Sleeping Dog. Up the right side, the same five again, because every stone had a twin. And on top, the Bite.” He tapped his front tooth. “Chipped it when I dropped it on my foot.”

“Did it hurt?”

“Nothing hurts here. I still said a word I won’t say.”

The fresh spawns giggled, so Pop whistled through his teeth and let his voice go low.

“When the stones were all up, I took the hill away. One brick at a time, from the middle.”

“And the arch fell down,” said the other fresh spawn.

“The arch stayed up,” said Pop. “Still does.”

That’s where everybody laughs, because Pop’s First Day stories grow about a stud every time he tells one. One detail always turns out to be true, but nobody knows which one until later.

My little sister, Tinsley, was hanging upside down off the end of the bench. She’s six. Today she was mostly glitter, plus the plain grey stripe she wears down the middle of every look now.

“POP DOESN’T LIE,” she yelled at the fresh spawns. “He just makes things BIGGER.”

“Thank you, Tins,” said Pop.

Mom came out of the Hut in her orange vest, with a stack of maps under one arm. “Welcome!” she said to the fresh spawns. Then she said it to Pop, and then, out of habit, to the bench.

My name’s Nolan Tibbs, and I’ve been in Spawnbury for all eleven of my years. I wear the Starter Look too: a cream T-shirt, plain blue pants and brown hair in three flat strips. They’re wavy as bacon in a hot pan. Folks call it bacon hair. Most of them hand me a map.
