Read the opening pages

Monster Homeroom is going north to see snow. Basil Bogwhistle is going to see an igloo.

Basil is witch-folk, so everybody expects him to carry potions, giggle at the worst moments and grow up to brew like his Nana. Basil wants to be a builder. On the morning of the class trip, he leaves all four of his bottles on a windowsill. That afternoon, he sneaks his best friends up the snowy slope to the igloo he wants for a clubhouse.

The storm comes early.

Strays appear out of the snowy plains with arrows that make your legs feel full of sand. Strips of powder snow lie across the only way back, smooth and waiting. Mr. Clattersby can't follow, because skeleton-folk never go near powder snow. And under the third carpet of the igloo, something is groaning.

Lexi has a plan. Pearl has pink boots. Rufus has a leather cap that fits nobody. And Basil is about to find out what a builder needs to carry.

Four friends take turns telling it. An unofficial winter survival adventure set in the blocky world of Minecraft, with real game rules.

Read the opening pages

Mr. Clattersby put a snowball in the item frame by the lectern. Thirty kids in Homeroom 6-M leaned forward like he had hung up a slice of cake.

It was a Wednesday, the coldest morning of the year so far, and everybody had come to school wrapped in their biggest scarves. Cold is one thing. Snow is another. Nobody in Oddstone has ever watched snow fall, because it doesn’t. Oddstone gets rain, and fog off the swamp, and on bad days both. The snow stays up north on the mountains, where it belongs.

“This,” said Mr. Clattersby, “came down the field-trip line last night, packed in ice inside a barrel. It is a snowball. On Friday, Monster Homeroom will go and see where it came from.”

The room went off like a dropped bundle of fireworks.

Pearl turned see-through from pure joy. Her hot-pink scarf and her hot-pink boots stayed exactly where they were, which is how you always know where Pearl is. Rufus stretched his huge arms over his head and groaned a happy zombie-folk groan that made Saffi Bonewick rattle. Lexi is the new kid and the only normal person in our whole school. She wrote FRIDAY: SNOW at the top of a clean page and underlined it twice.

I opened my sketchbook to the page that said CLUBHOUSE, SITE 5.

Here is my problem. For three months I have been trying to build a clubhouse for the four of us, and every site I pick gets “improved.” Site one became a pumpkin patch. The second one turned into a viewing deck. Site three is under the stage at the Playhouse, which is fine, if you like being two blocks tall. Site four is a watch post now, with a sign that says NO HOLES PERMITTED.

Principal Loftus improves things at night, and you never see her do it. You just come to school and the library is on a different floor.

Under SITE 5, I wrote: IGLOO??

“Settle,” said Mr. Clattersby. His bow tie was green, which meant it wasn’t a test day. “Cold places have their own rules, and you will learn them before we board a single cart. Rule one.”

He turned the page of the Mob Studies book on the lectern.

“Strays. A stray is a skeleton that got cold, and it lives in the snowy plains. It comes out in the dark, like any skeleton, and like any skeleton it shoots. But a stray’s arrows make you slow. Your legs go heavy, and slow is how you get hit by the second arrow. A stray is still a real skeleton, which makes it undead. The sunrise burns it, and a splash of Healing hurts it.”
