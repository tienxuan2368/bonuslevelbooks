Read the opening pages

Tolu is the best hider on any server. On the new map, that might be the problem.

The Jungle Dome is a giant glass greenhouse full of ferns, rope bridges and a tree so tall it touches the roof. Waiting inside is SnowPeek, a famous Chiller who always picks Snowfall: a cold snap that makes every Survivor who isn't hidden glow right through the walls.

Tolu always stays dark. But while she hides, Anika, Grace and Wilder end up frozen in frost pods, round after round. Then Mr. Pappas, the building super, plays the game for the very first time, draws the Chiller and wins by standing still. He never needed to find Tolu, he tells her, because she was never going anywhere.

Now three friends are stuck in three pods, the clock is running out, and the crew's quietest member has to do the scariest thing in the game: let the Chiller see her.

Read the opening pages

The Furnace Room was the hottest room in the Ironwood, and nobody would leave it.

It was the first Monday of summer vacation. Outside, the whole city was ninety-seven degrees. Down here, beside the old boiler, it felt like ninety-eight, and the four of them had come down anyway.

Mr. Pappas had carried down a box fan with one bent blade. It turned its head slowly from side to side, like it was looking for somebody. Every time it found Tolu Adeyinka, her two puffs of hair blew sideways and her round glasses slid down her nose.

Tolu was squashed into the corner of a saggy couch pulled up to the folding table. Her purple backpack was on, of course.

Even Radiator had given up on the warm pipes. The building’s enormous orange cat lay flat on the concrete like a rug somebody had spilled.

“Nobody touch me,” said Grace. She had a cold can of soda pressed to her neck, and she was drumming on it with her other hand. “If anybody touches me, I’ll melt, and you’ll have to mop me up.”

“I’d mop you,” said Tolu.

“Thank you, Tolu.”

The stairs thumped. Wilder Haskins came down with a dog leash still looped around his wrist. His red hair had given up standing and lay flat with sweat.

“It’s so hot my mom walks the dogs at seven in the morning now,” he said. “Seven. In the morning. On vacation. Waffles sat down in the middle of the sidewalk and refused to have opinions.”

“Waffles always refuses,” said Tolu.

“Today he refused harder.”

Anika Desai had pushed her big plastic watch halfway up her arm, off her sweaty wrist, and she still checked it every few seconds. “Seven-oh-one,” she said. “And there’s a new map. Look.”

She turned her laptop around. On the vote pad stood a giant bubble of glass with a jungle inside it. Leaves as big as doors hung over rope bridges that looped like string, and in the middle stood a tree so tall it touched the top.

“The Jungle Dome,” Anika read. “A greenhouse. Seven terminals, four pods, two Gates. Most of it is plants.”

“Finally,” said Wilder. “A map as hot as we are.”

“Glass walls,” Grace said slowly. “Glass roof. You can see in from everywhere.”

Tolu looked at the leaves. You could get under leaves.

She was the best hider in the Ironwood. In the game, she was the best hider on any server, and everybody knew it. When Tolu hid, nobody found her. Grace called it a superpower. Tolu thought it was more like a habit, the way some people bite their nails.

A toolbelt jingled on the stairs. Mr. Pappas came down holding a sweating glass of iced tea, with his reading glasses parked on top of his head.
