Read the opening pages

Something in Room 108 is eating the guests' treasures. And something on the back stair is waiting for a wooden door to give.

It's Lantern Week at the Grand Lantern Hotel, where every corner is lit, because nothing can appear in a lit room. Then a music disc vanishes from a guest's chest. A spyglass follows. Then a compass and a cookie disappear from a room whose door never opened.

Everyone blames the silent Wandering Trader, who only hums and turns invisible every night. Monty says he saw him do it. But ten-year-old bellhop Nettie Ferreira notices things, and the times on the front-desk Guest List don't add up.

With Yuki, who counts everything, Monty, who can get anyone talking, and her little brother Rory, who is sure every creak is a ghost, Nettie has two days to clear the Trader before he leaves forever. But the groan coming up through the floor of Room 108 is no ghost. And the lanterns on the back stair have been missing for two nights.

A spooky hotel mystery where every clue works the way it does in Minecraft, with a Spot-the-Clue Answer Key at the back.

Read the opening pages

THE first guest of Lantern Week walked up the Lantern Road at noon, carrying her trunk the way people carry a baby.

She did not let Flurry the snow golem anywhere near it, and she kept her chin over the lid the whole way.

“Good afternoon, One-Oh-Eight!” Great-Aunt Honoria sang from the Lantern Stair, sweeping down in her long burgundy coat. “You look like a woman who has carried that trunk a very long way.”

“Only from the ferry,” said the guest. “May I have a room close to the stairs? Oh. Never mind. Any room is fine.”

Behind the front desk, Nettie Ferreira straightened her cap, which was burgundy and round and exactly one size too big. She was ten years old, she was the junior bellhop, and she noticed things.

She noticed that the guest said “never mind” before anyone could answer.

The guest signed the Guest Book on the lectern by the stairs. Mrs. Zinnia Plover. Her letters were small and neat, and they leaned forward as if they were in a hurry to get somewhere.

Nettie pressed the button on the desk, and the polite bell went ding. It wasn’t really a bell, but a note block sitting on a gold block, which made it sound like one. The hotel had owned a real bell for exactly one day, sixty years ago. Every villager in Tallow Bend had run home to bed.

“Room 108 is ready,” Nettie said. “May I carry your trunk, Mrs. Plover?”

“Will you be very careful?” asked Mrs. Plover.

“Yes, ma’am.”

It wasn’t heavy at all. But Mrs. Plover watched it every step of the way, like a hen watching her only egg.

On the Lost & Found chest, Velvet the cat opened one blue eye, looked at the trunk, and closed it again.

• • •

Sixty-four lanterns hung over the Lantern Stair on long chains, and their light lay on every step like warm honey.

“The third step creaks,” Nettie said. “Everybody steps over it.”

Mrs. Plover stepped over it.

The guest floors began one flight above the Lobby. Room 108 was at the end of the first-floor hall. It had a bed, a checked carpet, a wooden chest, an ender chest and a lantern in every corner. Its window looked down on the Courtyard. It smelled a little of old sheets, the way it must have smelled long ago, when it was the linen room.

Nettie set the trunk down at the foot of the bed, and then she stopped.

The wooden chest stood beside the bed.

For as long as Nettie could remember, that chest had stood under the window, but now it didn’t. And the carpet around it was wrong, because the checks didn’t line up anymore. Burgundy, cream, cream, burgundy, as if someone had picked up part of the carpet and put it back down crooked. And two extra lanterns stood on the floor beside the chest.
