Read the opening pages

Every evening at sunset, the Home Note calls the fairies home. Tonight, it cannot ring.

Bluet is the smallest fairy in Dewlight Hollow, no taller than a poppy. One morning she finds a square hole in the moss, a block of moss sitting on a root where moss never grows, and Zaley's dandelion gone. Old Oxeye knows who did it: the Block-Taker, a tall enderman of the night who carries the Hollow away one block at a time. Never look it in the face. Not ever.

Then the Block-Taker sets a block of moss right on top of the little bell, and the Home Note goes silent. The rain is coming, and the nearest dry place is the fairies' own home.

Somebody will have to look up. And the oldest fairy in the Hollow has a story he has never finished.

Read the opening pages

Someone had taken a piece of the Hollow.

Bluet saw it the moment she opened her round door. Right beside the Home Note, there was a hole in the moss. It was square. It went down much deeper than Bluet was tall. At the bottom there was only bare brown dirt.

Bluet flew to the edge and looked down.

“Hello, little somebody?” she called.

Nobody answered. The hole was empty.

Nobody in Dewlight Hollow was smaller than Bluet. She was named for the azure bluet, and she stood no taller than one. Her skin was warm brown. Her sky-blue hair sat in two round puffs. She had four white petal wings. And she said hello to everything, even holes.

“That’s one hole,” said Beet, landing beside her. He pulled his red cap down tight. “One is a lot of holes, when it’s in the Hollow.”

Beet was Bluet’s best friend. He patted the six pockets of his moss-green coat, one by one. That was what he did when something was wrong. A rolled leaf map was tucked under his arm, and Beet counted everything.

Then a shout came from the other side of the Home Note.

“My dandelion!” It was Zaley. Her bright pink hair stuck up like a flower after a storm. “Somebody took my dandelion!”

The Plant-One-More bed grew in the moss beside the Home Note. A firefly bush stood there, and a prickly sweet berry bush. Between them there had been a bright yellow dandelion. Zaley had planted it herself.

Now there was only a little dark gap where it used to be.

“Rusk took it!” cried Zaley. “Rusk always takes things!”

Rusk was a red fox who lived across the stream. He loved to borrow things and carry them about in his mouth.

“Hmm, let me check,” said Beet. He walked all around the square hole. Then he pointed.

A block of moss sat on top of the Old Azalea’s biggest root. It sat up there like a hat on the wrong head. Moss had never grown on that root, not ever.

“That’s the moss from the hole,” said Beet. “A fox can carry a map in his mouth. But a fox can’t carry a whole block of moss. Nobody small did this.”

Bluet looked at the moss on the root. She looked at the gap in the flower bed.

“Somebody picked the moss up,” she said slowly. “Then they put it down again, somewhere silly. And then they took the dandelion instead.”

Chime came floating over to see what everyone was staring at. Chime was a little blue allay with see-through wings. It glowed softly, even in the daytime. It peered into the hole and chortled. Its chortle sounded like a giggle in a bottle.
