Read the opening pages

In the game, when the ground shakes, you run for open ground. In real life, you drop right where you are. And an earthquake never sends a warning.

Every Friday, four friends log in from the back room of a small-town hardware store and drop onto Rumbletop Island, a disaster-survival game on Roblox. Ingrid reads the sky. Booker jumps first. Zora names every disaster. And Cyrus, the quietest Lookout, always knows which building is about to fall.

This week the floor moves with no banner. The Lookouts' favorite trick wins round after round, until the Clockspire Library, where the open courtyard is where the clock spire comes down. Cyrus saw it coming. He just said it too quietly.

Then the real floor moves. Paint cans pour down the aisles, the lights go out, Booker's little sister is alone on the sidewalk, and Baro the dog has vanished. This time Cyrus has one second to say the real rule loud enough for everyone to hear.

Read the opening pages

FORECAST: Light snow ending. Indoor drill at 10:15. No banner expected.

Tumbleford Elementary. Tuesday. 10:14 a.m.

The winter’s first snow fell overnight, and it lies across the playground like a clean sheet that nobody has wrinkled.

Inside Ms. Tull’s science room, Ingrid Storvik is not looking at the snow, because she is busy watching the clock.

“One minute,” she whispers.

“How do you know?” whispers Booker Ellis.

“The office said ten fifteen, and the office is always early.”

The speaker over the door gives three long beeps.

“Drop, cover, hold on,” says Ms. Tull in her calm teacher voice. “This is a drill.”

Twenty-two kids hit the floor at once, chairs scraping, and somebody’s pencil case bursts open. Ingrid drops to her hands and knees and crawls under the black lab table. She grabs one of its thick metal legs, and her white-blond braid swings and settles.

Booker lands beside her with a thump. His red hoodie sleeves are pushed up and he is grinning, as always.

“In the game,” he whispers, “you run.”

“This isn’t the game.”

“In the game, the building falls on you.”

“That’s why it’s a game,” says Ingrid, counting under her breath. Four. Five. Six.

At the next table, Cyrus Amini holds a table leg with one hand. With the other, he pushes up his round glasses. His thick black hair sticks up in the back. He isn’t looking at Booker, though. He is studying the tall bookcase in the corner.

Two metal straps hold the top of the bookcase to the wall, and each strap has two screws.

“Anchored,” Cyrus says, to nobody. “Good.”

Ms. Tull walks between the tables in her snow boots. She runs the weather station on the school roof, and she does every drill as if it counts.

“Cover your head and neck, and hold on to the leg,” she says. “If the table slides, you slide with it.”

DISASTER DESK: Drop, Cover, and Hold On

Here’s the real science. When the ground shakes, the safest move is to stay right where you are. Drop onto your hands and knees. Cover your head and neck with your arms, or crawl under a sturdy table or desk. Hold on until the shaking stops. Don’t run outside while the ground is moving, and don’t stand in a doorway. A sturdy table covers you. A doorway doesn’t.

IN THE GAME: When the map shakes, players run for open ground.

IN REAL LIFE: Indoors, you drop, cover and hold on right where you are.

The speaker gives one short beep. All clear.

Twenty-two kids climb out from under the tables, and Ms. Tull waits until the chairs stop scraping.

“One question,” she says. “Why don’t we stand in a doorway?”
