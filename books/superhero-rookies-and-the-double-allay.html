Read the opening pages

One amethyst shard. One dancing allay. Pop: now there are two.

At Blockburst Academy, every student's superpower works like something real in the blocky world. Wendell Tran's power is Shell: twenty seconds of being very tough and very, very slow. His little sister Mai is the fastest kid in town, and she has never waited for anybody. At the Thaw Social, she hands the Fizzles' allay a shard while it dances, and now Mai has an allay of her own.

Then, at dusk, Mai leads a creeper away from two little kids and dives into a crack by the pond. Her new allay follows her down. Deep under the meadow waits the Rattle Room, where a monster cage wakes for anyone within sixteen blocks and keeps making skeletons for as long as she is near.

The grown-ups are too big to fit. So four rookie heroes go in. Their first plan fails. To bring his sister home, the slowest hero in the school will have to do the bravest thing he can think of: go first, and go slow.

Read the opening pages

The snow had melted off the meadow at last. So Blockburst Academy did what it always did when something good happened. It put a jukebox in the Great Hall and turned it up loud.

Once a year, for the Thaw Social, the folks of Stoneskip rode up the Rail. They wore their muddiest boots.

Lou Barrows, the cook, set out cookies, pumpkin pie and a cake with a candle on top. She added a whole barrel of sweet berries from the Trans’ patch by the pond. She did not set out milk. She never set out milk.

“Eat first,” Lou told everybody. “Dance second.”

Over the big doors hung a brand-new sign from Headmistress Lowell.

THAW SOCIAL.

DANCE NICELY.

NO BURSTING

ON THE FLOOR.

Rafi Okafor looked at Lou’s cake. It had no lit candle on it anymore, because three seconds earlier he had sneezed in the middle of a gust.

“In my defense,” he said, “that cake was very close to my face.”

Rafi was ten. He had short black twists that always blew backward, a sky-blue tunic with white swirls on it and a long orange scarf. His Burst was Gust. It worked like a wind charge, and it came in fours. After four puffs he was empty until he’d had a rest and a snack.

He had three puffs left and a brand-new idea.

“Tonight,” he announced, “I’m going by the Big Bluster.”

“No,” said a small voice by his elbow.

That was Mai Tran. She was seven. Her short black hair stuck up in the back no matter what anybody did to it.

Her berry-red tunic had pockets lumpy with sweet berries. She was the fastest kid in Stoneskip. Nobody had ever seen her walk.

“No?” said Rafi.

“No,” said Mai, and zipped off toward the cookies.

Frankie Ramirez stood by the cookie plate with both hands full. Cyan swirls drifted off her lime-green tunic. Her Burst was Swift, and she mostly used it to reach food first.

“Six cookies,” she reported. “Ten seconds. Personal best.”

“ENTER GIGI, STAGE LEFT!”

Gigi Larsen spun onto the dance floor in a purple tunic and a tall magenta cap. Pale swirls puffed up, and Gigi vanished. Her cap floated on by itself and took a deep bow.

Then there was Wendell Tran.

Wendell was eleven and built tall and broad. He wore round glasses and a turtle shell on his head. He danced the way he did everything: slowly and carefully, without stepping on anybody. Mai had come back and was standing on his boots, which helped.

“You’re so slow,” Mai told him happily.

“Slow is how you don’t step on people,” Wendell said.

High above the jukebox, a little blue allay was dancing.
