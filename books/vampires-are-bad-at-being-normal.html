Read the opening pages

Mina Nightshade has one goal this week: be normal. That is very hard when you are an eleven-year-old vampire.

The mayor of Hollowbrook has announced the village's first-ever Sunrise Parade. Every family must spend four nights building a parade arch, then walk to the lake at dawn and take off their hats for the first sunbeam. For the Nightshades, who sneeze little puffs of smoke in the sun and hide iron helmets inside their hats, this is a disaster. And they have been given the place of honor, facing the sunrise.

So Mina writes her Rules for Passing as Normal. Her grandmother leaves cryptic notes. Her little brother keeps trying to turn into a bat and turning into a chicken instead. Her nosy neighbor is collecting evidence in a bottle. And her new friend Kofi has started to notice things, like a lantern in the lake that nobody is holding.

Then the whole sleepless village starts to attract a storm of phantoms, and only a family that truly knows the night can save Hollowbrook. But first, Mina has to stop being normal.

Read the opening pages

Mayor Dumbleford Pruitt has never had a good idea at a good time.

He once held a picnic in a thunderstorm. He once held a snowball contest in a village with no snow. Then, one hot afternoon, he rang the bell and ruined my whole life.

Ding-dong.

In Hollowbrook, the bell is how important things start. Late in the day, the villagers always gather around it with their big noses and their little hums.

“Hmm,” said the Farmer.

“Hrmm,” agreed the Librarian.

Then the Mayor rang the bell, and every villager ran straight home, the way villagers always do when a bell rings.

After that came the rest of us, the townsfolk, the ones who talk. I stood at the very back, under the biggest hat in Hollowbrook. It was as wide as a cake, with a purple band around it. It also had an iron helmet hidden inside, which is not a normal thing for a hat to have.

My name is Wilhelmina Nightshade, but everyone calls me Mina. I am eleven years old. I have a black bob with one white streak, red-violet eyes, and a short black cape with a crimson lining. My family lives in the creaky dark-oak manor on top of Raven Hill.

We are vampires.

When this story starts, only five people in the whole world know that. Six, if you count the cat, and seven, now that you know. So please act normal.

“Hollowbrook-ians!” shouted the Mayor from on top of a hay bale. He is a round man with a round voice, and his hat is shaped like a smaller, prouder hat. “I have had an idea!”

Somebody groaned. It may have been me.

“For one hundred years, this village has slept through every single sunrise,” said the Mayor. “No more! In four mornings, we will greet the sun together, at the first-ever… SUNRISE PARADE!”

The townsfolk clapped. Behind a closed door somewhere, a villager hummed. My stomach did a slow, sad flip, like a fish that has given up.

Things my family is bad at: sunrises, parades, and clapping at the right time.

“Now, as you all know, Hollowbrook does not have wheels,” the Mayor went on.

This is true. Nobody here has ever seen a wheel, because everything in Hollowbrook is square. The sun is square, and so is the moon. A wheel would be shocking.

“So instead of floats, every family will build a parade arch over the lane to the lake!” said the Mayor. “We’ll call it the Parade Road. We’ll build at night, by lantern light, so every arch is a surprise. Four nights of building! Then, in the last hour before sunrise, we’ll all walk the road together, under every arch, down to Moonmirror Lake.”
