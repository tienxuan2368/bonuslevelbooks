Read the opening pages

The whole town says the Librarian stole the Golden Apple. The Librarian can only say "hmm."

For a hundred years, the Golden Apple of Riverlatch has hung untouched on the Library wall. Then, two days before Book Day, the frame is empty, the old Library door has been smashed and replaced, and the Librarian is selling a shy new girl an enchanted book for one emerald. Is he paying for her silence? If the apple isn't back by Book Day, the Librarian goes downriver for good.

Jory Pennant writes everything down. Imani tests everything with redstone and trusts numbers more than people. Wynn knows what every mob will do next. Wallop, a one-horned goat, charges anything that stands still. Together they stake out the Library in the dark, and the dark answers back: a zombie in the reading room, boats on the steps, drowned in a thunderstorm and, at midnight, a zombie siege.

Every clue works the way it does in Minecraft, and before the reveal the book stops to ask: CAN YOU SOLVE IT?

Read the opening pages

WHEN: Sunrise · WHERE: Library Lane

Every morning, in the last hour before sunrise, my dad walks the lanes of Riverlatch and says good morning to the lamps. He is the Keeper of the Lamps. The lamps switch themselves on at dusk and off at sunrise, all by themselves, and Dad takes this a little personally.

That morning I walked with him, because it was Book Week, and I wanted to be first at the Library. Book Day was two days away.

It had rained all night, the way it nearly always does in Riverlatch. Just before sunrise it stopped, for once, and the wet cobblestones shone orange under every lamp.

“Morning, Number Fourteen,” Dad said to the lamp at the corner of Library Lane. “Lovely work last night. Don’t listen to anybody who says—“

He stopped walking.

A thin gray thread of smoke was rising from the Library steps. On the bottom step lay a lump of rotten flesh.

“Something was standing there when the sun came up,” Dad said quietly. “It isn’t standing anymore.”

Fact: zombies burn when the sun touches them. Fact: when they burn, they leave rotten flesh behind.

So. A zombie had been on the Library steps at sunrise, inside the town.

I opened the Case Book and wrote it down. I keep a clock on a string and a spyglass in my coat, but the Case Book is my real tool. Smoke. Rotten flesh. Library steps. Sunrise.

I was still writing when the rotten flesh blinked out of the world, the way dropped things do after about five minutes. Then the rain started again, and there was only rain.

Imani Brooks was sitting on the top step, under the little roof by the door, with her satchel in her lap. Her raincoat is teal and smudged red with redstone dust, and her hair is in two puffs with orange ribbons.

“Seventy-one,” she said, without looking up.

“Seventy-one what?”

“Drops off the gutter since I sat down. I was early.”

Then Wynn Tolliver came running up the lane in her yellow boots, talking before she reached us. Wynn is nine, small and freckled, and she runs everywhere.

“Did you know zombies burn at sunrise, which is why there’s smoke, and was that rotten flesh, and also good morning?”

Behind her trotted Wallop, our goat, who has one horn, a name tag and a lot of opinions. He looked at the smoke, and then he looked at me, so I stepped sideways, out of his way. With Wallop, it is safest not to stand still.

That is the club: three detectives and a goat. Our headquarters is an old signal box by the rails, and the sign over its door says CLUES, NOT GUESSES.
