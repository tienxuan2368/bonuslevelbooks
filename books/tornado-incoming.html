Read the opening pages

In the game, you run out of the building. In real life, you get inside. Ingrid Storvik just mixed them up.

Every Friday at 3:30, four friends log in from the back room of a small-town hardware store and drop onto Rumbletop Island, a disaster-survival game on Roblox. Ingrid has a plan for everything. Booker has a balloon and zero patience. Cyrus knows which roof will fall. Zora names every storm before it arrives.

They are eleven rounds into their best streak ever when the banner flashes INCOMING: TORNADO. Ingrid does exactly what you should do in real life: get inside, get low, cover your head. On Rumbletop, the funnel tears the diner apart around her, and the streak is gone.

A week later, the sky over their town turns green-gray. The radio says watch, then warning. The siren starts while two of the Lookouts are on a bus, the lights go out, and Ingrid's dog is missing. Now the real rule is the only one that matters.

Read the opening pages

FORECAST: Clear skies. Light winds. Streak: hot.

Amini Hardware, Main Street. The last Friday in April. 3:30 p.m.

The whiteboard in the Back Room says two things.

BEST: 12!!!

NOW: 11!!!!!

Booker Ellis wrote both lines, because Booker does not believe in one exclamation mark when five will fit.

“One more round ties the record,” he says. He is already in his chair with his red hoodie sleeves shoved up and his sneakers bouncing under the table. “Two more and we break it. Two rounds. Easy.”

“Nothing is easy,” says Ingrid Storvik. She hangs her yellow raincoat on the hook, even on a blue-sky day. A weather notebook pokes out of the pocket, the way it always does.

Cyrus Amini comes in from the store with a box of nails and pushes his round glasses up his nose. “Two rounds is not easy,” he says. “Two rounds is two rounds.”

Zora Ellis is eight, and she is under the table in her dinosaur backpack. The yellow beads on her cornrows click against a table leg whenever she moves.

“Why are you under there?” Ingrid asks.

“It’s a bunker,” says Zora. “In case.”

Out front, Grandpa Javad’s slow voice talks to somebody about potting soil. A weather radio is clipped to his belt, as always.

The pretzel jar sits in the middle of the table, where everybody can reach it and Booker can reach it first.

Ingrid opens her notebook. She has been reading about tornadoes all week. In Tumbleford spring means storms, and her mom works long shifts at the hospital. Ingrid likes to know what to do before anybody has to tell her.

At the top of a clean page, in her neatest letters, she has written a rule.

TORNADO: GET INSIDE. GET LOW. COVER YOUR HEAD.

She underlines it twice and cracks her knuckles. “Log in,” she says.

/ / /

On Rumbletop Island, the Gondola hangs in a sky so blue it looks painted. It is a hot-air-balloon basket as big as a gym, and on the Lookouts’ private server it carries exactly four players.

NimbusNotebook wears a yellow rain hat and carries a clipboard. AirTimeAce has a red hoodie and sunglasses parked on his head. BoltsAndBeams wears a hard hat and a tool belt. LittleHailstone is very small, with a white helmet and a cape made from a towel.

Every player gets one piece of gear per round. Booker grabs a balloon, because he always grabs a balloon, and Zora grabs one because Booker did. Cyrus and Ingrid each take an apple.

“Apples heal,” Ingrid says. “Balloons pop.”

Far below, the island sits in a flat blue sea. On the highest rocks stands the Old Lighthouse, red and white and all alone. It is the first thing they look for after every drop, and it is the reason they call themselves the Lighthouse Lookouts.
