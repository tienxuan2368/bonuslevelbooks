A Funny Monster School Mystery for Kids 9-12

Read the opening pages

At a school where nobody is normal, the normal kid is the weird one.

Lexi Plainfield is eleven, brand-new in town, and completely, totally normal. Everyone else in Homeroom 6-M has a thing. Pearl turns see-through when she's embarrassed. Rufus sinks like a stone and always has something useful in his pockets. Basil sparkles purple when he thinks hard and giggles at the worst possible moments. So on her very first morning, Lexi panics and tells the whole class she's witch-folk. On her aunt's side. She doesn't have an aunt.

Then the school bell starts ringing at midnight, every single night. It wakes the villagers. It wakes the whole town. And Principal Loftus says that if nobody can explain it by Friday sunset, the Harvest Fair is cancelled. The town blames the skeleton-folk archery teacher. The class president blames the new family. And Lexi's lie is getting harder to keep.

Four friends take turns telling one wild week, with a fair mystery where every clue is on the page. An unofficial adventure set in the blocky world of Minecraft, with barrel lockers, a parrot who copies monsters and a square moon over the bell tower.

Read the opening pages

Okay. Plan. One: walk into Homeroom 6-M like a normal kid. Two: sit near the back, but not so far back that it looks like I’m hiding. Three: do not tell anyone about my light map.

I made the plan the night before, along with my light map. I’ll explain the map later. (It isn’t weird, or at least it’s only a little weird.)

The plan lasted until the door.

The door to Homeroom 6-M was made of iron, with no handle at all, just a stone button beside it the size of a cookie.

“You have to push the button,” said a cheerful voice behind me.

I turned around, but nobody was there.

Well, a pink scarf was there, hanging in the air at about shoulder height all by itself, with its long knitted ends swinging. Below it, two hot-pink boots stood on the stone floor, and above it there was nothing at all.

“Sorry,” said the nothing. “I always go see-through when I’m excited, and you’re new, and new is very exciting.”

The air above the scarf began to shimmer. A girl faded in slowly, the way frost creeps across a window. She had silvery-blue skin with a soft glow, white hair in a high ponytail with a pink scrunchie, and an enormous grin.

“I’m Pearl Wispington,” she said. “I’m ghost-folk, I’m in the drama club, and someday I’m going to be a star. You must be the Plainfield girl.”

“I’m Lexi,” I said. “How did you already know who I am?”

“Everybody knows. You live over the map shop on the square, and your dad has volunteered for four different things since Sunday.” She pressed the button, and the iron door swung open with a heavy clunk. “Welcome to 6-M. Everybody calls it Monster Homeroom, and we take that as a compliment. After you, Lexi Plainfield.”

¶ ¶ ¶

“Why is the door made of iron?” I asked as we went in.

“Because wooden doors don’t last very long around here,” said Pearl. “You’ll see. The lockers are barrels, too, because a chest won’t open if somebody’s cat is sitting on it, and somebody’s cat is always sitting on it.”

Inside 6-M, a cyan parrot sat on a perch by the window. A fat book called Mob Studies lay open on a lectern at the front.

And every single kid in the room had a thing.

I knew about mob-folk, because Mom had explained them on the long minecart ride here. “In Oddstone, most families have a little bit of a mob in them,” she’d told me. “It’s like how some families are all tall, or all left-handed.” Out in the far plains villages where we used to live, mob-folk were just a story. Here, they were a whole homeroom.
