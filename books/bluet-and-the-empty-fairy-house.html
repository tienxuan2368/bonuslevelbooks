Read the opening pages

How do you say hello to someone who can't see you?

There's a new girl in the village up the hill. Her name is Ellie, and she has nobody to play with. At the bottom of her grandma's garden sits a tiny old fairy house that no fairy has ever moved into.

Bluet and her fairy friends want to make Ellie feel welcome. But fairies stay hidden from big folk. And speedy Zaley, who makes friends by zooming straight at them, is about to learn that some friends run when you chase them.

So the fairies try the quiet way. With careful Beet and his leaf signs, sleepy Old Oxeye, Chime the little blue allay who brings back everything, a slice of cake and one shy baby rabbit, Bluet sets out to make the empty fairy house not empty anymore.

Read the opening pages

“A new girl?” said Old Oxeye. “Hmm-hmm. Now then.”

Old Oxeye sat in his nook beside the Home Note. It was the very heart of Dewlight Hollow. He was the oldest fairy in the Hollow. He was round and slow, with a white beard as fluffy as a daisy. He wore a yellow cap. His walking stick was cut from sugar cane.

All around him, the Hollow was busy. Fairies came and went through little round doors in the roots of the Old Azalea. Glow berries hung over every door. The Home Note was a note block on a block of gold, with nothing but air on top. Every evening at sunset, Old Oxeye rang it, and every fairy flew home.

Next to the Home Note grew a little firefly bush. It was the first plant in the Plant-One-More bed.

A soft blue glow drifted by. That was Chime, the fairies’ friend. Chime was an allay, a small flying helper with see-through wings. If you gave Chime one thing, it brought back everything like it. Everything.

Down below the Hollow, Duffle the big sniffer was sniffing for seeds in his Digging Patch. Snuff-snuff.

Chime floated over to Bluet. It made its happy sound, like a giggle in a bottle.

“Hello, Chime,” said Bluet. “We have news. Big news.”

“Tell it from the start,” said Old Oxeye. “Slowly. For Chime, and for my old ears.”

“Her name is Ellie,” said Bluet. “She’s Mrs. Mabry’s granddaughter. She just came to live in Brightmeadow. And she said there’s nobody to play with.”

“I wanted to say hello,” said Zaley. “Beet wouldn’t let me.”

“Fairies stay hidden from big folk,” said Beet. “That’s the rule.”

“Hmm-hmm,” said Old Oxeye. He pointed his stick at an old sign by the Hollow’s biggest door.

NEW HERE?

WELCOME!

WIPE YOUR WINGS.

— by order of Old Oxeye

“When someone new comes to the Hollow,” said Old Oxeye, “we welcome them.”

“How?” asked Bluet.

“The meadow’s way,” said Old Oxeye. “Softly. Kindly. From a little way off.”

Zaley frowned. “Softly is hard.”

“Hmm-hmm. I know.” Old Oxeye’s eyes twinkled. “But kindness can go places a fairy can’t. It can go to a big girl who doesn’t even know you’re there. And it can still say hello.”

“How?” asked Beet. “How do you say hello without saying hello?”

“You leave something kind where they will find it,” said Old Oxeye. “And then you let them find it. That’s the hard part. The letting.”

“Like a flower on a doorstep?” asked Bluet.

“Just like that,” said Old Oxeye.

“I could leave a flower!” said Zaley. “I could leave a hundred flowers! I could leave them really fast!”

“Hmm-hmm,” said Old Oxeye. “Or really kindly.”
