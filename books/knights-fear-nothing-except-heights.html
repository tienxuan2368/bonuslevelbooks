Read the opening pages

Nine knights looked at the iron golem in the cage on Grumble Tower. Not one of them ever went to get him.

Pebblecrown is the smallest kingdom in the blocky world: one castle, one village, one bell, and three squires who want to be knights more than anything. Loud Hildy quotes the Squire's Code at everyone. Careful Kiran reads banners the way other kids read books. And gentle, enormous Alfie apologizes to everything, has never struck anything in his life, and has never once asked for something he wanted.

Until now.

Alfie wins one day, from sunrise to sunset, to bring the caged golem home. But the way to the tower runs past a patrol of crossbow-carrying pillagers, up a cliff that turns out to terrify a knight, and onto ground where the Grumbles never run out. And the moment the cage opens, the golem doesn't follow Alfie at all. It charges straight into the fight.

A daring, funny quest novel for kids 9-12, set in a Minecraft kingdom where every golem, lead, fence and pillager outpost works just like the game. Short chapters, no gore, battles against the game's monsters only, and a story about honor and teamwork: a gentle squire learns that leading means going first and looking back.

Read the opening pages

Hear now the tale of the iron golem who stood in a cage on the far ridge, and of the gentle squire who went to get him.

It began, the way most things in Pebblecrown begin, with somebody apologizing to a chicken.

“Sorry,” said Alfie Thatch. “Sorry. I didn’t see you there.”

The chicken had been asleep on the Wall Walk, the long stone path along the top of the castle wall. Alfie had nearly stepped on it in the gray light before sunrise. It looked at his enormous boots. Then it looked at the rest of him, which went up a long way. Then it walked off, making the small, offended noises of a chicken with somewhere better to be.

Alfie was twelve, and the tallest person in the realm. He had warm brown skin, curly black hair and round cheeks, and his green leather tunic had sheep’s wool stuck all over it, because it always did. His family kept the sheep of the Sheepwalk, and he carried the biggest plain shield in the castle. He had never struck anything with anything in his whole life.

Behold Alfred the Gentle Giant of the Sheepwalk, who walked the battlements of the mighty realm at dawn, keeping watch over—

“I’m just tall,” said Alfie.

The Chronicler withdraws “Giant.” Alfie was tall. He was keeping watch.

It was the dawn watch. After the raid at Midsummer, the Steward had decided that the castle should keep one. So two squires walked the wall every morning and looked at the realm before it woke up. The realm was easy to look at. It was not very big.

Kiran Mehta was already at the far corner, leaning on the battlement with a short copper tube held to one eye.

Kiran was eleven, with brown skin, sharp eyebrows and neat black hair, except for one tuft that refused. A new stick of charcoal sat behind his ear. His blue tunic had a pocket full of dyes, and his fingers were stained purple at the tips, the way they always were after a day at the loom.

“What’s that?” said Alfie.

“A spyglass. Nani’s.” Kiran didn’t lower it. “Copper, and a shard of amethyst at the end. She uses it to read banners a long way off. She lent it to me, as long as I don’t drop it.”

“Are you reading banners?”

“I’m reading clouds.” Kiran swung the spyglass slowly across the sky. “That one’s a Chevron.”

Alfie looked. The cloud was a flat white triangle, wide at the bottom, sitting on the far hills like a tent. It did look like a Chevron. Every pattern the loom could weave had a name, and Kiran knew all forty-three of them, and he used them for everything.
