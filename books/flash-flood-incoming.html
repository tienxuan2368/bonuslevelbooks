Read the opening pages

In the game, you jump to beat the water. In real life, you turn around. Booker Ellis is about to learn the difference.

Every Friday, four friends log in from the back room of a small-town hardware store and drop onto Rumbletop Island, a disaster-survival game on Roblox. Ingrid reads the sky. Cyrus knows which roof will hold. Zora names every disaster. And Booker jumps first, every single time.

Now the banner says INCOMING: FLASH FLOOD, and Booker's jumps keep costing the team. The water climbs, the streak dies, and one round leaves him stranded alone on a sinking roof.

Meanwhile, after four days of November rain, the real river is rising. A flash flood warning crackles over the radio. The lights go out, the store's basement starts to fill, and Booker's little sister is stuck on the far side of the Low Bridge, where the road has vanished under brown water.

Read the opening pages

FORECAST: Rain, heavy at times. Flood watch through Saturday evening.

The rain gauge on the Storvik porch is a clear plastic tube with numbers up the side. Ingrid Storvik crouches in front of it in her yellow raincoat and reads it the way a nurse reads a thermometer.

Four inches.

That is four days of rain. Monday was a drizzle, Tuesday was steady, and Wednesday never stopped. Today the rain falls in long gray ropes, and the gutter over the steps sounds like a running faucet.

Ingrid empties the tube, sets it back on the railing and opens her weather notebook. She writes with her braid tucked inside her hood, so it won’t drip on the page.

3:12 p.m. 4.1 inches since Monday. Wind from the east. Sky: low and gray, no edges.

Barometer lies on his rug by the door. He is a scruffy gray terrier with one ear up and one ear down. He watches the rain the way a guard watches a gate.

“Come on, Baro,” Ingrid says. “Mom’s on a night shift. You’re coming to the store.”

Baro sighs. He has been to the store on storm days before, and he knows exactly where the bathtub is.

In the kitchen, the weather radio clicks on with a long tone. A calm computer voice reads a flood watch for the Tumble River area.

Ingrid stands very still and listens to the whole thing, and then she writes one more line, in capitals.

FLOOD WATCH. GET READY.

DISASTER DESK: What Does a Flood Watch Mean?

Here’s the real science. A flood watch means flooding is possible. That is the time to get ready: check the family kit and know where the nearest higher ground is. A flood warning, or a flash flood warning, means flooding is happening or is about to happen. That is the time to act right away and move to higher ground. The short version: watch means be prepared, warning means take action.

IN THE GAME: The yellow banner gives about ten seconds before the water starts to climb.

IN REAL LIFE: When a flood warning comes, move to higher ground right away.

/ / /

Main Street has puddles the size of parking spaces, and Ingrid and Baro go around every one.

The bell over the door of Amini Hardware jingles. A pallet of sandbags by the entrance is half gone, and behind the counter, Grandpa Javad looks up over his reading glasses. His silver mustache is neat. His gray cardigan has a pencil in the pocket, and his weather radio is clipped to his belt.

“Watch, not warning,” he says, before Ingrid can ask. “So we get ready. Are you ready?”

“I have four inches and a plan,” says Ingrid.
