Books for kids who love video games

Updated November 7, 2026 · By the Bonus Level Books team · Save to Pinterest

A lot of kids who say they hate reading will happily explain the rules of their favorite game for twenty minutes. That's a reader in disguise. They already love quests, levels, secrets and a hero who fails and tries again, which are the same things that make a chapter book hard to put down.

Here's how to get a gamer reading, and where to start.

Five ways to get a gamer reading

  1. Start with their game. A story set in a world they already know removes the hardest part: caring about a strange new place. Minecraft fans can start with our Minecraft books by age, Roblox fans with our Roblox chapter books.
  2. Make reading a game. Our free Reading Quest printable turns every 15 minutes of reading into a block to color and a level to clear.
  3. Read chapter one out loud together. Once they're hooked, step back and let them finish on their own.
  4. Let them be the expert. Ask what the book got right about the game. Kids love catching a detail a grown-up missed.
  5. Pick books with something to do. Solve-it-yourself mysteries, code-breakers and quizzes at the back give a gamer a goal.

Questions parents ask

Do video game books count as real reading?

Yes. A chapter book builds vocabulary, stamina and the habit of reading for fun, whatever it's about. A story in a familiar world is often what gets a reluctant reader to finish a whole book for the first time.

Does my child need to play the games?

No. Each book explains what a reader needs to know as the story goes.

Are these official game books?

No. They are unofficial stories with original characters, not affiliated with or endorsed by Mojang, Microsoft, Roblox Corporation or any game's creators.
